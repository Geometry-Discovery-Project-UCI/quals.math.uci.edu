## Part A

::ProblemBlock{number=A1}
#problem
For
$$
\varepsilon y''+y'-x^\beta y=0,\qquad 0<x<1,\qquad y(0)=y(1)=1,
$$
where $0<\varepsilon\ll1$ and $\beta>-1$, find a leading order boundary-layer approximation.
#proof
The reduced outer problem is
$$
y'-x^\beta y=0.
$$
Thus
$$
y_{out}=C\exp\left(\frac{x^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right).
$$
The convection coefficient of $y'$ is positive, so the outer solution satisfies the right boundary condition $y(1)=1$. Hence
$$
C=\exp\left(-\frac1{\beta+1}\right),
\qquad
y_{out}=\exp\left(\frac{x^{\beta+1}-1}{\beta+1}\right).
$$
The remaining condition at $x=0$ is enforced by a layer of thickness $O(\varepsilon)$. Put $X=x/\varepsilon$ and $y=Y(X)$. The leading layer equation is
$$
Y''+Y'=0,
$$
so $Y=A+Be^{-X}$. Matching gives $A=y_{out}(0)=e^{-1/(\beta+1)}$, and $Y(0)=1$ gives $B=1-e^{-1/(\beta+1)}$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{y_c(x)=\exp\left(\frac{x^{\beta+1}-1}{\beta+1}\right)+\left(1-e^{-1/(\beta+1)}\right)e^{-x/\varepsilon}.}
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=A2}
#problem
Use the contraction mapping theorem for $\dot u=f(u)$ with $f$ locally Lipschitz; give a continuous nonunique example; state smooth dependence if $f\in C^k$.
#proof
Let $X=C^0([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],\mathbb{R}^n)$ and
$$
(Tu)(t)=u_0+\int_0^t f(u(s))\,ds.
$$
On the ball $\left\lVert u-u_0\right\rVert_\infty<\delta$, suppose $|f|\le M$ and $f$ is Lipschitz with constant $K$. If $\varepsilon M<\delta$ and $\varepsilon K<1$, then $T$ maps the ball into itself and
$$
\left\lVert Tu-Tv\right\rVert_\infty\le\varepsilon K\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_\infty.
$$
Thus $T$ has a unique fixed point, which is exactly the IVP solution.

For nonuniqueness with continuous $f$, take
$$
\dot u=\sqrt{|u|},\qquad u(0)=0.
$$
Both $u(t)=0$ and $u(t)=t^2/4$ for $t\ge0$ solve the IVP. If $f\in C^k$, then the flow depends $C^k$ on the initial data and is as smooth in time as the equation permits.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=A3}
#problem
For
$$
\dot x=x(1-3x^2-4y^2)-2y,
\qquad
\dot y=y(1-4x^2-3y^2)+2x,
$$
show existence of a periodic orbit, give Floquet multipliers in integral form, and determine stability.
#proof
The radial equation satisfies
$$
\frac12\frac d{dt}(x^2+y^2)=x\dot x+y\dot y
=x^2+y^2-3x^4-3y^4-8x^2y^2.
$$
Thus $\dot r>0$ for $r$ small and $\dot r<0$ for $r$ large. Also the angular velocity is bounded away from zero in the annular trapping region. By the Poincare--Bendixson theorem, a periodic orbit exists.

Let the periodic orbit be $(x_*(t),y_*(t))$ with period $T$. One Floquet multiplier is $1$. The product of the multipliers equals
$$
\exp\left(\int_0^T\operatorname{div}F(x_*(t),y_*(t))\,dt\right).
$$
Here
$$
\operatorname{div}F=2-12x^2-12y^2.
$$
Hence the nontrivial multiplier is
$$
\boxed{\mu_2=\exp\left(\int_0^T(2-12x_*^2(t)-12y_*^2(t))\,dt\right).}
$$
Since the orbit is attracting in the radial direction, this integral is negative; therefore $|\mu_2|<1$, and the periodic orbit is locally asymptotically stable.

::ProblemBlock{number=B1}
#problem
Analyze the multirate explicit Euler method in the exam and apply it to $x'=-x+y$, $y'=-y$.
#proof
The method uses two half steps for $x$ and one full step for $y$. Taylor expansion shows the $x$ update is locally second order at integer times when $y$ is treated at the prescribed multirate level; the $y$ update is the usual explicit Euler update.

For the linear system,
$$
y_{k+1}=(1-h)y_k.
$$
Also
$$
x_{k+1/2}=x_k+\frac h2(-x_k+y_k),
$$
$$
x_{k+1}=x_{k+1/2}+\frac h2(-x_{k+1/2}+y_k).
$$
Therefore
$$
x_{k+1}=\left(1-h+\frac{h^2}{4}\right)x_k+\left(h-\frac{h^2}{4}\right)y_k.
$$
The amplification matrix is triangular with eigenvalues
$$
1-h,
\qquad
1-h+\frac{h^2}{4}.
$$
Both have modulus less than $1$ precisely when
$$
\boxed{0<h<2.}
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=B2}
#problem
State the least squares normal equations, uniqueness criteria, residual uniqueness, and solve the exam's minimum-norm example.
#proof
A least squares solution satisfies
$$
A^T(Ax-b)=0.
$$
It is unique iff $\ker A=\{0\}$, i.e. $A$ has full column rank. The residual $r=Ax-b$ is always unique because $Ax$ is the orthogonal projection of $b$ onto $\mathcal R(A)$.

For
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad b=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},
$$
the exact solution set is $x=(s,1-s,-s)$. Minimize
$$
\left\lVert x\right\rVert^2=s^2+(1-s)^2+s^2=3s^2-2s+1.
$$
The minimizer is $s=1/3$, hence
$$
\boxed{x_*=(1/3,2/3,-1/3)^T.}
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=B3}
#problem
Prove $\operatorname{tr}A$ equals the sum of eigenvalues, derive the relative error estimate for $Ax=b$, and discuss ill-conditioning of
$$
A_\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1+\epsilon\end{pmatrix}.
$$
#proof
The coefficient of $\lambda^{n-1}$ in $\det(\lambda I-A)$ is $-\operatorname{tr}A$. Since the same polynomial is $\prod_j(\lambda-\lambda_j)$, the coefficient is also $-\sum_j\lambda_j$. Hence $\operatorname{tr}A=\sum_j\lambda_j$.

If $A(x+\delta x)=b+\delta b$, then $A\delta x=\delta b$, so
$$
\left\lVert \delta x\right\rVert\le\left\lVert A^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert \delta b\right\rVert.
$$
Since $\left\lVert b\right\rVert\le\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert x\right\rVert$,
$$
\frac{\left\lVert \delta x\right\rVert}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\le \kappa(A)\frac{\left\lVert \delta b\right\rVert}{\left\lVert b\right\rVert}.
$$
For $A_\epsilon$, $\det A_\epsilon=\epsilon$. One eigenvalue is near $2$ and the other is near $\epsilon/2$, so $\kappa(A_\epsilon)=O(1/\epsilon)$. Thus it is ill-conditioned for small $\epsilon$.

## Part C

::ProblemBlock{number=C1}
#problem
Find the shortest path on the unit sphere between two points $a$ and $b$.
#proof
The minimizer is the shorter great-circle arc joining $a$ and $b$. In spherical coordinates the length functional is
$$
\int\sqrt{\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2}\,dt.
$$
The Euler--Lagrange equations are the geodesic equations for the round sphere. Their solutions are great circles, because a unit-speed geodesic has acceleration normal to the sphere. The plane through $0,a,b$ intersects the unit sphere in a great circle, and the shorter arc has length
$$
\arccos(a\cdot b),
$$
which is the spherical distance. Hence it is the global minimum.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=C2}
#problem
For a frictionless harmonic oscillator, write the Lagrangian, Euler--Lagrange equation, Hamiltonian system, and solve for position.
#proof
The Lagrangian is
$$
L=\frac12m\dot x^2-\frac12kx^2.
$$
Euler--Lagrange gives
$$
m\ddot x+kx=0.
$$
The momentum is $p=m\dot x$, and
$$
H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12kx^2.
$$
Hamilton's equations are
$$
\dot x=\frac pm,
\qquad
\dot p=-kx.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{x(t)=A\cos(\sqrt{k/m}\,t)+B\sin(\sqrt{k/m}\,t).}
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=C3}
#problem
Assume $|F''|\le\lambda_1$, the first Dirichlet eigenvalue. If $u_0$ solves the Euler--Lagrange equation for
$$
I(u)=\int_\Omega\left(\frac12|\nabla u|^2+F(u)\right)dx,
$$
prove it is a minimum.
#proof
Let $u=u_0+v$, $v\in H_0^1(\Omega)$. Taylor's theorem gives
$$
F(u_0+v)=F(u_0)+F'(u_0)v+\frac12F''(u_0+\theta v)v^2.
$$
The first variation term vanishes because $u_0$ satisfies the Euler--Lagrange equation. Hence
$$
I(u)-I(u_0)=\frac12\int_\Omega |\nabla v|^2+F''(u_0+\theta v)v^2\,dx.
$$
Using $F''\ge-\lambda_1$ and Poincare's inequality,
$$
I(u)-I(u_0)\ge\frac12\int_\Omega |\nabla v|^2-\lambda_1v^2\,dx\ge0.
$$
Thus $u_0$ is a minimizer.
