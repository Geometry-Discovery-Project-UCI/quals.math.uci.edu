::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Let $T:V\to V$ be linear. If $v,w$ are eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues, prove that $\{v,w\}$ is linearly independent.

#proof
Suppose $av+bw=0$, where $Tv=\lambda v$, $Tw=\mu w$, and $\lambda\neq\mu$. Applying $T$ gives

$$
a\lambda v+b\mu w=0.
$$

Subtracting $\mu(av+bw)=0$ yields

$$
a(\lambda-\mu)v=0.
$$

Since $v\neq0$ and $\lambda-\mu\neq0$, we get $a=0$. The original relation then gives $b=0$ because $w\neq0$. Thus the two vectors are linearly independent.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
**(a)** For an odd prime $p$, prove that there are exactly two groups of order $2p$ up to isomorphism.

**(b)** Find the order of a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $GL_n(\mathbb F_p)$.

#proof
**(a)** If $|G|=2p$, then the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups divides $2$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo $p$, so the Sylow subgroup $P\cong C_p$ is normal. By Cauchy's theorem there is a subgroup $Q\cong C_2$, and $G=P\rtimes Q$. The action $Q\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)\cong C_{p-1}$ is either trivial or has image the unique element of order $2$, namely inversion. The trivial action gives $C_{2p}$; inversion gives the dihedral group of order $2p$. These are not isomorphic because one is abelian and the other is not.

**(b)** We have

$$
|GL_n(\mathbb F_p)|=prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(p^n-p^j)
=p^{0+1+\cdots+(n-1)}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(p^{n-j}-1).
$$

The second product is not divisible by $p$. Hence a Sylow $p$-subgroup has order

$$
p^{n(n-1)/2}.
$$

The upper unitriangular matrices form a subgroup of exactly this order.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let $A,B\triangleleft G$. If $G/A$ and $G/B$ are abelian, prove that $G/(A\cap B)$ is abelian.

#proof
Since $G/A$ is abelian, every commutator of elements of $G$ lies in $A$. Similarly every commutator lies in $B$. Therefore

$$
[G,G]\subseteq A\cap B.
$$

A quotient $G/N$ is abelian exactly when $[G,G]\subseteq N$. Taking $N=A\cap B$ proves that $G/(A\cap B)$ is abelian.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
**(a)** Let $H\leq S_p$ act transitively on $\{1,\ldots,p\}$, where $p$ is prime. Prove that $H$ contains a $p$-cycle.

**(b)** Give a transitive subgroup of $S_4$ containing no $4$-cycle.

#proof
**(a)** By orbit-stabilizer,

$$
|H|=p\,|H_1|,
$$

so $p$ divides $|H|$. Cauchy's theorem gives an element of order $p$ in $H$. A nonidentity permutation of order $p$ in $S_p$ must consist of one $p$-cycle, so it is a $p$-cycle.

**(b)** The Klein four group

$$
V_4=\{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}
$$

acts transitively on four letters. All its nonidentity elements have order $2$, so it contains no $4$-cycle.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
For every $f(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$, prove that the principal ideal $(f(x))$ is not maximal in $\mathbb Z[x]$.

#proof
The zero ideal is not maximal because $\mathbb Z[x]$ is not a field, and the unit ideal is not a proper ideal. Suppose first that $f$ is a nonconstant nonunit. Choose a prime $p$ not dividing its leading coefficient. Then the reduction $\overline f\in\mathbb F_p[x]$ is nonconstant. The ideal

$$
(f)\subsetneq(f,p)\subsetneq\mathbb Z[x]
$$

is proper because its quotient is $\mathbb F_p[x]/(\overline f)\neq0$, and the first inclusion is strict because a nonzero constant $p$ cannot be a multiple of the nonconstant polynomial $f$.

If $f=n$ is a nonunit constant, choose a prime $p$ dividing $n$. Then

$$
(n)\subseteq(p,x)\subsetneq\mathbb Z[x].
$$

The maximal ideal $(p,x)$ strictly contains $(n)$, since $x\notin(n)$. Thus no principal ideal is maximal.

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Let $F$ be a field with $7^3$ elements. Prove that $3$ is not a square in $F$.

#proof
The prime subfield is $\mathbb F_7$. The nonzero squares in $\mathbb F_7$ are

$$
1,2,4,
$$

so $3$ is not a square there. If $3$ had a square root in $F=\mathbb F_{7^3}$, then $F$ would contain

$$
\mathbb F_7(\sqrt3)\cong\mathbb F_{7^2}.
$$

But the subfields of $\mathbb F_{7^3}$ are $\mathbb F_{7^d}$ with $d\mid3$, and $2\nmid3$. Hence $\mathbb F_{7^2}$ is not a subfield of $F$, a contradiction.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $p$ be prime and $\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}$.

**(a)** For which $p$ does $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ contain a Galois extension $E/\mathbb Q$ with Galois group $C_3$?

**(b)** Prove that every such $E$ is contained in $\mathbb R$.

#proof
**(a)** The Galois group of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)/\mathbb Q$ is cyclic of order $p-1$. It has a quotient of order $3$, and hence a corresponding degree-$3$ Galois subfield, exactly when

$$
3\mid p-1.
$$

Thus the answer is precisely the primes $p\equiv1\pmod3$.

**(b)** Complex conjugation restricts to an automorphism of $E/\mathbb Q$ whose order divides $2$. But $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$ has order $3$ and therefore has no nonidentity element of order $2$. Conjugation must act trivially on $E$, so every element of $E$ is real and $E\subseteq\mathbb R$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Let $R$ be an integral domain and $M$ an $R$-module. Define

$$
\operatorname{Tor}(M)=\{m\in M:rm=0\text{ for some }0\neq r\in R\}.
$$

**(a)** Prove that $\operatorname{Tor}(M)$ is a submodule.

**(b)** Prove that $M/\operatorname{Tor}(M)$ is torsion-free.

#proof
**(a)** If $rm=0$ and $sn=0$, with $r,s\neq0$, then

$$
rs(m+n)=s(rm)+r(sn)=0.
$$

Since $R$ is a domain, $rs\neq0$, so $m+n$ is torsion. Also, if $a\in R$, then $r(am)=a(rm)=0$, so $am$ is torsion. Additive inverses are immediate. Hence $\operatorname{Tor}(M)$ is a submodule.

**(b)** Suppose $0\neq r\in R$ and

$$
r(m+\operatorname{Tor}(M))=0.
$$

Then $rm$ is torsion, so $srm=0$ for some $s\neq0$. Since $sr\neq0$, this means $m$ is torsion. Therefore $m+\operatorname{Tor}(M)=0$, proving that the quotient is torsion-free.

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
For each item, give an example or prove none exists.

**(a)** A simple group $G$ with a subgroup $H$ that is not simple.

**(b)** A nonabelian group for which $g\mapsto g^2$ is a homomorphism.

**(c)** A real $3\times3$ matrix $M$ satisfying $M^3=-I_3$.

**(d)** A field and a polynomial whose splitting field is not Galois.

#proof
**(a)** Take $G=A_5$ and

$$
H=\{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}\cong V_4.
$$

The group $A_5$ is simple, while the nontrivial abelian group $V_4$ is not simple.

**(b)** No such group exists. If squaring is a homomorphism, then

$$
(xy)^2=x^2y^2.
$$

Thus $xyxy=xxyy$, and cancellation gives $yx=xy$ for all $x,y$. Hence the group is abelian.

**(c)** One example is $M=-I_3$, since $(-I_3)^3=-I_3$.

**(d)** In characteristic $p$, take the base field $k=\mathbb F_p(t)$ and

$$
f(x)=x^p-t.
$$

Its splitting field is $k(t^{1/p})$. This is a nontrivial purely inseparable extension, so it is not separable and therefore is not Galois.
