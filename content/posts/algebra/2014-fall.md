::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Define each of the following: $S_n$, ring, ring homomorphism, field, and PID.

#proof
(a) The symmetric group $S_n$ is the group of all bijections from $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ to itself, with composition as its operation.

(b) A ring is an abelian group under addition together with an associative multiplication that distributes over addition. Under the convention used here, rings have a multiplicative identity.

(c) A ring homomorphism $\phi:R\to S$ is a map preserving addition, multiplication, and identity:
$$
\phi(a+b)=\phi(a)+\phi(b),\quad
\phi(ab)=\phi(a)\phi(b),\quad
\phi(1_R)=1_S.
$$

(d) A field is a nonzero commutative ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.

(e) A principal ideal domain is an integral domain in which every ideal is generated by one element.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Let $F$ be a field. Prove that the additive group $(F,+)$ and the multiplicative group $(F^*,\cdot)$ are not isomorphic.

#proof
If $\operatorname{char}F=0$, the additive group is torsion-free, whereas $-1\in F^*$ has order $2$. Hence the groups are not isomorphic.

Suppose $\operatorname{char}F=p>0$. Every nonzero element of $(F,+)$ has order $p$. But $F^*$ has no element of order $p$: if $x^p=1$, then in characteristic $p$,
$$
(x-1)^p=x^p-1=0,
$$
so $x=1$. Thus the two groups have different $p$-torsion. This also covers $p=2$; when $F=\mathbb F_2$, the additive group has two elements while $F^*$ has one. Therefore the groups are never isomorphic.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let $R$ be a PID and let $P$ be a nonzero prime ideal. Prove that $P$ is maximal.

#proof
Write $P=(p)$ with $p\ne0$. Suppose
$$
(p)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R.
$$
Then $p=ab$ for some $b\in R$. A generator of a nonzero prime ideal is a prime element and hence irreducible. Therefore either $a$ is a unit, in which case $(a)=R$, or $b$ is a unit, in which case $(a)=(p)$. There is no ideal strictly between $(p)$ and $R$, so $P$ is maximal.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Prove that no group of order $132$ is simple.

#proof
Since $132=2^2\cdot3\cdot11$, the number of Sylow $11$-subgroups is $1$ or $12$. If it is $1$, that subgroup is normal.

Assume $n_{11}=12$. The twelve Sylow $11$-subgroups have pairwise trivial intersections and contribute
$$
12(11-1)=120
$$
nonidentity elements. The number of Sylow $3$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_3\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad n_3\mid44,
$$
so $n_3$ is $1$, $4$, or $22$. The value $22$ is impossible because it would contribute $22(3-1)=44$ additional nonidentity elements, while only $11$ remain.

If $n_3=1$, the Sylow $3$-subgroup is normal. If $n_3=4$, conjugation on the four Sylow $3$-subgroups gives a nontrivial homomorphism $G\to S_4$. Were $G$ simple, this map would be injective, but $132$ does not divide $|S_4|=24$. Hence its kernel is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup. Thus $G$ is not simple in every case.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Determine the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
$$
x^4+x^2+1
$$
and its degree over $\mathbb Q$.

#proof
We have
$$
x^4+x^2+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2-x+1).
$$
The roots are the primitive third and sixth roots of unity. They all lie in
$$
\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}).
$$
Conversely, a root of either quadratic generates $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Thus the splitting field is
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}),
$$
and its degree over $\mathbb Q$ is $2$.

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Find two $4\times4$ matrices with the same characteristic and minimal polynomials that are not similar.

#proof
Let
$$
A=J_2(0)\oplus J_2(0)
$$
and
$$
B=J_2(0)\oplus J_1(0)\oplus J_1(0).
$$
Both matrices have characteristic polynomial $x^4$. Their largest Jordan blocks have size $2$, so both have minimal polynomial $x^2$. But their Jordan partitions are $(2,2)$ and $(2,1,1)$, respectively. Hence their Jordan canonical forms differ and they are not similar.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
(a) Let $R$ be an integral domain. Prove that
$$
R[x]^*=R^*.
$$

(b) Give a ring $R$ and nonconstant $f(x),g(x)\in R[x]$ such that $f(x)g(x)=1$.

#proof
(a) If $f(x)g(x)=1$ in $R[x]$, then because $R$ is a domain,
$$
\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g.
$$
The product has degree $0$, so both polynomials are constant. Their constant values multiply to $1$, so each lies in $R^*$. Conversely, every unit of $R$ is plainly a unit in $R[x]$.

(b) Take $R=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ and
$$
f(x)=1+2x,
\qquad g(x)=1-2x.
$$
Both are nonconstant, and
$$
f(x)g(x)=1-4x^2=1
$$
in $R[x]$.

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Let $p$ be prime. Prove that the Galois group of $x^p-2$ over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to
$$
\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
:a,b\in\mathbb F_p,\ a\ne0
\right\}.
$$

#proof
Let $\alpha=\sqrt[p]{2}$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $p$th root of unity. The splitting field is
$$
L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta).
$$
The polynomial $x^p-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, so $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=p$. Also $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=p-1$. The intersection of these fields has degree dividing both $p$ and $p-1$, hence is $\mathbb Q$. Therefore
$$
[L:\mathbb Q]=p(p-1).
$$

Every automorphism is determined by
$$
\sigma_{a,b}(\zeta)=\zeta^a,
\qquad \sigma_{a,b}(\alpha)=\zeta^b\alpha,
$$
where $a\in\mathbb F_p^*$ and $b\in\mathbb F_p$. All $p(p-1)$ choices occur. Composition satisfies
$$
\sigma_{a,b}\sigma_{c,d}=\sigma_{ac,,ad+b},
$$
which is exactly the multiplication law
$$
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}c&d\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}ac&ad+b\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Thus the stated map is a group isomorphism.

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Determine all real matrices $A$ with characteristic polynomial
$$
x^3(x^2+1)
$$
up to similarity over $\mathbb R$.

#proof
The factor $x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb R$ and occurs with multiplicity one, so it contributes the unique real companion block
$$
C=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}.
$$
For the zero eigenvalue, the Jordan-block sizes form a partition of $3$. There are exactly three partitions: $(3)$, $(2,1)$, and $(1,1,1)$. Hence the three similarity classes are represented by
$$
J_3(0)\oplus C,
$$
$$
J_2(0)\oplus[0]\oplus C,
$$
and
$$
[0]\oplus[0]\oplus[0]\oplus C.
$$
These are pairwise nonsimilar because their minimal polynomials are respectively
$$
x^3(x^2+1),\qquad x^2(x^2+1),\qquad x(x^2+1).
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $p$ be prime and let $r,N$ be positive integers. For the map
$$
\phi:\mathbb F_{p^r}^*\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{p^r}^*,
\qquad x\longmapsto x^N,
$$
find the cardinalities of its kernel and image.

#proof
The multiplicative group $\mathbb F_{p^r}^*$ is cyclic of order
$$
m=p^r-1.
$$
If $g$ is a generator, then $g^k$ lies in the kernel exactly when
$$
m\mid Nk.
$$
There are precisely $\gcd(N,m)$ residue classes $k$ modulo $m$ satisfying this condition. Therefore
$$
|\ker\phi|=\gcd(N,p^r-1).
$$
By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$
|\operatorname{im}\phi|
=\frac{p^r-1}{\gcd(N,p^r-1)}.
$$
