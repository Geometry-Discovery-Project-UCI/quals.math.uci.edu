::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Let $D_{2n}$ be the dihedral group of order $2n$.

(a) Prove that for an odd prime $p$, a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $D_{2n}$ is normal and cyclic.

(b) If $2n=2^\alpha k$ with $k$ odd, prove that $D_{2n}$ has $k$ Sylow $2$-subgroups and describe them.

#proof
Write
$$
D_{2n}=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
$$

(a) The quotient $D_{2n}/\langle r\rangle$ has order $2$. Any odd-order subgroup maps trivially to this quotient, so every $p$-subgroup lies in the cyclic rotation group $\langle r\rangle$. A cyclic group has a unique subgroup of each possible order. Thus its Sylow $p$-subgroup is cyclic and characteristic in $\langle r\rangle$, hence normal in $D_{2n}$.

(b) Since $n=2^{\alpha-1}k$, the rotation $r^k$ has order $2^{\alpha-1}$. For $0\le j<k$, put
$$
P_j=\langle r^k,sr^j\rangle.
$$
Each $P_j$ is dihedral of order $2^\alpha$, so it is a Sylow $2$-subgroup. Every Sylow $2$-subgroup contains the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup $\langle r^k\rangle$ of the rotation group and some reflection $sr^j$. Two such groups coincide exactly when their indices are congruent modulo $k$. Therefore the complete list is
$$
P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_{k-1},
$$
and there are $k$ of them.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Let $G$ be a group such that $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is cyclic. Show that $G$ is abelian.

#proof
The inner automorphism group
$$
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)
$$
is a subgroup of the cyclic group $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, so $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic. If $G/Z(G)$ is generated by $gZ(G)$, every element of $G$ has the form $g^az$ with $z\in Z(G)$. Any two such elements commute. Hence $G$ is abelian.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
(a) Determine whether
$$
\mathbb F_5[x]/(x^2+1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb F_5[x]/(x^2+2)
$$
are isomorphic.

(b) List all ideals of $\mathbb Z[x]/(2,x^3+1)$.

#proof
(a) The polynomial $x^2+1$ splits over $\mathbb F_5$ because $2^2=-1$, so
$$
\mathbb F_5[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb F_5\times\mathbb F_5.
$$
On the other hand, $x^2+2$ has no root because $-2=3$ is not a square modulo $5$. Thus
$$
\mathbb F_5[x]/(x^2+2)\cong\mathbb F_{25}
$$
is a field. The rings are not isomorphic.

(b) We have
$$
\mathbb Z[x]/(2,x^3+1)
\cong\mathbb F_2[x]/((x+1)(x^2+x+1))
\cong\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_4.
$$
Each factor is a field, so the four ideals are
$$
(0),\quad A,quad(\overline{x+1}),
\quad(\overline{x^2+x+1}),
$$
where $A=\mathbb Z[x]/(2,x^3+1)$. Under the product decomposition, the last two are $0\times\mathbb F_4$ and $\mathbb F_2\times0$, respectively.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Prove that the Galois group of $x^5-2$ over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to
$$
\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
:a,b\in\mathbb F_5, a\ne0
\right\}.
$$

#proof
Let $\alpha=\sqrt[5]{2}$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive fifth root of unity. The splitting field is
$$
L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta).
$$
Eisenstein's criterion gives $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=5$, while $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=4$. Their intersection has degree dividing both $5$ and $4$, so it is $\mathbb Q$. Hence $[L:\mathbb Q]=20$.

Every automorphism has the form
$$
\sigma_{a,b}(\zeta)=\zeta^a,
\qquad
\sigma_{a,b}(\alpha)=\zeta^b\alpha,
$$
with $a\in\mathbb F_5^*$ and $b\in\mathbb F_5$, and all $20$ choices occur. Composition gives
$$
\sigma_{a,b}\sigma_{c,d}=\sigma_{ac,ad+b},
$$
which agrees with multiplication of the displayed matrices. Thus the correspondence is an isomorphism.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Let $F$ have characteristic not dividing $n$. Show that the equation
$$
XY-YX=I_n
$$
has no solutions in $n\times n$ matrices over $F$.

#proof
For any two square matrices over a commutative field,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(XY)=\operatorname{Tr}(YX).
$$
Taking traces of the proposed equation would give
$$
0=\operatorname{Tr}(XY-YX)=\operatorname{Tr}(I_n)=n\cdot1_F.
$$
This contradicts the assumption that the characteristic of $F$ does not divide $n$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Let $T$ act on a finite-dimensional rational vector space $V$. Suppose
$$
T^{15}=I
$$
and neither $T^3$ nor $T^5$ has a nonzero fixed vector. Show that $8$ divides $\dim V$.

#proof
Over a splitting field, $T$ is diagonalizable because $x^{15}-1$ has distinct roots in characteristic zero. Every eigenvalue has order dividing $15$. If an eigenvalue had order dividing $3$, its eigenvector would be fixed by $T^3$; if its order divided $5$, that vector would be fixed by $T^5$. Hence every eigenvalue has order exactly $15$.

Therefore the minimal polynomial and characteristic polynomial of $T$ are products of copies of the irreducible cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{15}(x)$, whose degree is
$$
\varphi(15)=8.
$$
Equivalently, $V$ is a vector space over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{15})$. Thus
$$
8\mid\dim_{\mathbb Q}V.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $A$ be a finite abelian group, let $p\mid|A|$, and let $p^k$ be the largest power of $p$ dividing $|A|$. Prove that
$$
(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A
$$
is isomorphic to the Sylow $p$-subgroup of $A$.

#proof
Write $A=S\oplus T$, where $S$ is the Sylow $p$-subgroup and $|T|$ is prime to $p$. Since
$$
(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z)\otimes A\cong A/p^kA,
$$
we compute the quotient. The group $S$ is annihilated by $p^k$, while multiplication by $p^k$ is an automorphism of $T$. Hence
$$
A/p^kA\cong S/p^kS\oplus T/p^kT\cong S.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
For complex irreducible representations of $S_4$:

(a) Show there are exactly two of degree $1$.

(b) Show the remaining degrees are $2,3,3$.

#proof
(a) One-dimensional characters factor through the abelianization. Since
$$
S_4/[S_4,S_4]=S_4/A_4\cong C_2,
$$
there are exactly two: the trivial and sign characters.

(b) The group $S_4$ has five conjugacy classes, corresponding to the five partitions of $4$, so it has five irreducible complex representations. Thus three degrees remain. The sum of the squares of all irreducible degrees is $|S_4|=24$. After the two linear characters, the remaining squares sum to
$$
24-1-1=22.
$$
Each remaining degree is at least $2$, and the only way to write $22$ as a sum of three squares of integers at least $2$ is
$$
22=2^2+3^2+3^2.
$$
Hence the remaining degrees are $2,3,3$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Let $R$ be a commutative local ring with maximal ideal $M$.

(a) Show that if $x\in M$, then $1-x$ is invertible.

(b) The printed question asserts that if $R$ is also Noetherian and $I^2=I$, then $I=0$.

#proof
(a) If $1-x$ were not a unit, it would lie in the unique maximal ideal $M$. Then
$$
1=(1-x)+x\in M,
$$
a contradiction. Thus $1-x$ is a unit.

(b) As printed, the assertion is false because $I=R$ satisfies $I^2=I$. The intended assertion is true for a proper ideal $I$. In that case $I\subseteq M$. Since $R$ is Noetherian, $I$ is finitely generated, and
$$
I^2=I
$$
implies $MI\supseteq I^2=I$, while always $MI\subseteq I$. Hence $MI=I$. Nakayama's lemma gives $I=0$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field. Show that every $x\in\mathbb F_q$ is a sum of two squares.

#proof
If $q$ is even, Frobenius $y\mapsto y^2$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb F_q$, so every $x$ is itself a square and
$$
x=y^2+0^2.
$$

Suppose $q$ is odd. The set of squares, including zero, has size $(q+1)/2$. Let
$$
S=\{y^2:y\in\mathbb F_q\}.
$$
The translate $x-S=\{x-z^2:z\in\mathbb F_q\}$ has the same size. Since
$$
|S|+|x-S|=q+1>q,
$$
the two subsets intersect. Thus $y^2=x-z^2$ for some $y,z$, and hence
$$
x=y^2+z^2.
$$
