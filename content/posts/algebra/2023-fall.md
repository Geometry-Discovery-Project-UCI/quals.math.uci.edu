For each action of $S_4$, identify the indicated stabilizer.

**(a)** The natural action on $\{1,2,3,4\}$; stabilize $2$.

**(b)** Left multiplication on $S_4$; stabilize $(23)$.

**(c)** Conjugation on $S_4$; stabilize $(23)$.

#proof
**(a)** The stabilizer consists of the permutations of $\{1,3,4\}$ that fix $2$:

$$
\operatorname{Stab}(2)=\langle(13),(14)\rangle\cong S_3.
$$

**(b)** The left-regular action is free, so

$$
\operatorname{Stab}((23))=\{e\}.
$$

**(c)** The stabilizer is the centralizer of $(23)$. Its elements may independently interchange $2,3$ and $1,4$, so

$$
C_{S_4}((23))=\langle(23),(14)\rangle
=\{e,(23),(14),(14)(23)\}.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Let $G$ be a group whose automorphism group is cyclic. Prove that $G$ is abelian.

#proof
The inner automorphisms form a subgroup

$$
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G).
$$

Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic, so $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$ is cyclic. Since

$$
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G),
$$

the quotient $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic. A group whose quotient by its center is cyclic is abelian. Indeed, if $G/Z(G)$ is generated by $gZ(G)$, then every element is $g^m z$ with $z\in Z(G)$, and any two such elements commute. Hence $G$ is abelian.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let $R$ be a nonzero finite commutative ring with identity. Prove that every prime ideal of $R$ is maximal.

#proof
Let $\mathfrak p$ be prime. Then $R/\mathfrak p$ is a finite integral domain. Every finite integral domain is a field: for $0\neq a$, multiplication by $a$ is injective and hence, on a finite set, surjective, so $ab=1$ for some $b$. Therefore $R/\mathfrak p$ is a field, which means $\mathfrak p$ is maximal.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
All ring homomorphisms are required to preserve identity.

**(a)** Describe all ring homomorphisms $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$.

**(b)** Describe all ring homomorphisms $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$.

#proof
**(a)** There is exactly one. A unital homomorphism must send $1$ to $(1,1)$ and therefore sends

$$
n\longmapsto(n,n).
$$

**(b)** Let $e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(0,1)$. They are idempotents, $e_1e_2=0$, and $e_1+e_2=1$. The only idempotents in $\mathbb Z$ are $0$ and $1$. Thus a unital homomorphism must send exactly one of $e_1,e_2$ to $1$ and the other to $0$. This gives precisely the two coordinate projections

$$
(a,b)\longmapsto a
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(a,b)\longmapsto b.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Let $R$ be a ring and $M$ an $R$-module. Prove that $M$ is finitely generated if and only if it is isomorphic to a quotient of a finite-rank free $R$-module.

#proof
If $M$ is generated by $m_1,\ldots,m_n$, define

$$
\varphi:R^n\longrightarrow M,
\qquad
(r_1,\ldots,r_n)\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^n r_jm_j.
$$

This map is surjective, so the first isomorphism theorem gives

$$
M\cong R^n/\ker\varphi.
$$

Conversely, if $M\cong R^n/N$, then the images of the standard basis vectors of $R^n$ generate the quotient. Hence $M$ is finitely generated.

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Does there exist a $14\times14$ integer matrix of order $49$?

#proof
No. Suppose $A^{49}=I$. Over characteristic zero, $x^{49}-1$ has no repeated roots, so $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$, and the order of $A$ is the least common multiple of the orders of its eigenvalues. If $A$ has order $49$, at least one eigenvalue must be a primitive $49$th root of unity. Its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ is $\Phi_{49}(x)$, whose degree is

$$
\varphi(49)=49-7=42.
$$

Therefore the minimal polynomial of $A$ has degree at least $42$. But the minimal polynomial of a $14\times14$ matrix has degree at most $14$, a contradiction.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Prove that $x^5-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$.

#proof
Let $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ and let $\mathcal O_K$ be its ring of integers. The rational prime $2$ is unramified in the cyclotomic extension $K/\mathbb Q$, because only the prime $5$ ramifies. Thus, for any prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathcal O_K$ lying over $2$,

$$
v_{\mathfrak p}(2)=1.
$$

The polynomial $x^5-2$ is therefore Eisenstein at $\mathfrak p$: its constant term lies in $\mathfrak p$ but not in $\mathfrak p^2$, and all other nonleading coefficients lie in $\mathfrak p$. Eisenstein's criterion in the discrete valuation ring $(\mathcal O_K)_{\mathfrak p}$ proves that $x^5-2$ is irreducible over $K$.

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Find the cardinalities of the following subsets of $\mathbb F_{3^6}$:

**(a)** $\{x^5:x\in\mathbb F_{3^6}\}$.

**(b)** $\{x\in\mathbb F_{3^6}:x^3=x\}$.

#proof
**(a)** The multiplicative group has order

$$
3^6-1=728.
$$

Since $\gcd(5,728)=1$, exponentiation by $5$ is an automorphism of this cyclic group. Every nonzero element is therefore a fifth power, and $0=0^5$. The cardinality is $3^6=729$.

**(b)** We have

$$
x^3-x=x(x-1)(x+1).
$$

These three roots are distinct in characteristic $3$, so the set is $\{0,1,-1\}$ and has cardinality $3$.

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Suppose $K/F$ is Galois and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\cong Q_8$. Can $K$ be the splitting field of a degree-$4$ polynomial over $F$?

#proof
No. If a degree-$4$ polynomial had splitting field $K$, the Galois group would act faithfully on its distinct roots. Thus $Q_8$ would embed in $S_m$ for some $m\leq4$, and hence in $S_4$.

An order-$8$ subgroup of $S_4$ would be a Sylow $2$-subgroup. Every Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$ is isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_8$, not to $Q_8$. For example, $S_4$ contains no copy of $Q_8$ because $Q_8$ has six elements of order $4$, whereas a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $S_4$ has only two. Hence the required faithful action cannot exist.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $p$ be prime, let $K$ be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of an irreducible polynomial $f$ of degree $p$, and let $\alpha,\beta$ be distinct roots such that

$$
\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta).
$$

Prove that $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$.

#proof
Put $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)$. Since $f$ is irreducible of degree $p$,

$$
[L:\mathbb Q]=p.
$$

The assignment $\alpha\mapsto\beta$ defines a $\mathbb Q$-embedding of $L$ into an algebraic closure. Because $\beta$ generates the same field, its image is $L$, so this is a nonidentity automorphism of $L/\mathbb Q$. Hence

$$
1<|\operatorname{Aut}(L/\mathbb Q)|\leq[L:\mathbb Q]=p.
$$

The automorphism group order divides the degree, so it has order $p$. Therefore $L/\mathbb Q$ is Galois. It contains every conjugate of $\alpha$, so it contains every root of $f$ and must equal the splitting field $K$. Thus $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)|=p$, and every group of prime order is cyclic.
