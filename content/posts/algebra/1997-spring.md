::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
If $N$ is a finite normal subgroup of $G$ and $G/N$ contains an element of order $n$, prove that $G$ contains an element of order $n$.

#proof
Let $gN$ have order $n$. Then $g^n\in N$, so $g^n$ has finite order because $N$ is finite. Hence $g$ has finite order $d$, and the order of its image $gN$ divides $d$, so $n\mid d$. The element $g^{d/n}$ then has order $n$.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $G$ be a finite simple group having exactly $2p+1$ Sylow $p$-subgroups. Prove that its Sylow $p$-subgroups are abelian.

#proof
Conjugation gives an action of $G$ on the set of its $2p+1$ Sylow $p$-subgroups. The kernel is normal. The action is nontrivial, and simplicity therefore makes it faithful. Thus every Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ embeds as a $p$-subgroup of $S_{2p+1}$.

The largest power of $p$ dividing $(2p+1)!$ is $p^2$, because
$$
v_p((2p+1)!)=\left\lfloor\frac{2p+1}{p}\right\rfloor=2
$$
for odd $p$. Hence $|P|\le p^2$. Every group of order $p$ or $p^2$ is abelian, so $P$ is abelian.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Suppose $G'\subseteq Z(G)$. For fixed $g\in G$, prove that
$$
f_g(x)=xgx^{-1}g^{-1}
$$
is a homomorphism $G\to G$.

#proof
The value $f_g(x)=[x,g]$ lies in $G'\subseteq Z(G)$. The commutator identity gives
$$
[xy,g]=x[y,g]x^{-1}[x,g].
$$
Because $[y,g]$ is central, this becomes
$$
[xy,g]=[y,g][x,g]=[x,g][y,g].
$$
Therefore $f_g(xy)=f_g(x)f_g(y)$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
In $R=\mathbb Z[X,Y]$, determine whether each ideal is prime and whether it is maximal:
$$
(a)\ (X,Y),\quad
(b)\ (X,3Y),\quad
(c)\ (X^2+1,Y),
$$
$$
(d)\ (3,X^2+1,Y),\quad
(e)\ (5,X^2+1,Y).
$$

#proof
(a) The quotient is $\mathbb Z$, an integral domain but not a field. Thus the ideal is prime but not maximal.

(b) The quotient is $\mathbb Z[Y]/(3Y)$. The nonzero classes of $3$ and $Y$ have product zero, so it is not a domain. The ideal is neither prime nor maximal.

(c) The quotient is $\mathbb Z[X]/(X^2+1)\cong\mathbb Z[i]$, a domain but not a field. Thus the ideal is prime but not maximal.

(d) The quotient is
$$
\mathbb F_3[X]/(X^2+1).
$$
The quadratic has no root in $\mathbb F_3$, so the quotient is a field. The ideal is maximal and prime.

(e) Modulo $5$,
$$
X^2+1=(X-2)(X+2),
$$
so the quotient has zero divisors. The ideal is neither prime nor maximal.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity. Suppose the coefficients of each of $f(X),g(X)\in R[X]$ generate the unit ideal. Prove that the coefficients of $f(X)g(X)$ also generate the unit ideal.

#proof
Let $I$ be the ideal generated by the coefficients of $fg$. If $I\ne R$, place it inside a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Reducing modulo $\mathfrak m$, we obtain
$$
\overline f\,\overline g=0
$$
in the polynomial ring over the field $R/\mathfrak m$. Since that polynomial ring is a domain, either $\overline f=0$ or $\overline g=0$. Thus all coefficients of $f$, or all coefficients of $g$, lie in $\mathfrak m$, contradicting the assumption that each coefficient ideal is $R$. Hence $I=R$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
How many elements of multiplicative order $9$ are in $\mathbb F_{64}$?

#proof
The multiplicative group $\mathbb F_{64}^*$ is cyclic of order $63$. Since $9\mid63$, it has a unique subgroup of order $9$. A cyclic group of order $9$ has
$$
\varphi(9)=9-3=6
$$
generators, so exactly six elements have order $9$.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $M$ be generated over $\mathbb Z$ by $a,b,c$, with relations
$$
4a+3b+3c=0,
\qquad
2a-b+3c=0.
$$
Express $M$ as a direct sum of cyclic modules.

#proof
The relation matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}4&3&3\\2&-1&3\end{pmatrix}.
$$
It has rank $2$. The gcd of its entries is $1$, and its $2\times2$ minors are $-10,6,12$, whose gcd is $2$. Thus its Smith normal form is
$$
\operatorname{diag}(1,2,0).
$$
Therefore
$$
M\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
$$
The cyclic summands have orders infinite and $2$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Find the minimal polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ of a primitive complex twentieth root of unity.

#proof
It is the twentieth cyclotomic polynomial. Since
$$
\Phi_{20}(x)=\Phi_{10}(x^2),
$$
we obtain
$$
\Phi_{20}(x)=x^8-x^6+x^4-x^2+1.
$$
This polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by the cyclotomic-polynomial theorem.

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Let $E$ be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of $X^{42}-1$. Determine the number of subfields of $E$.

#proof
We have $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{42})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{21})$ and
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)
\cong(\mathbb Z/42\mathbb Z)^*
\cong C_2\times C_6
\cong C_2\times C_2\times C_3.
$$
The $2$-primary group $C_2^2$ has five subgroups: the trivial subgroup, three subgroups of order $2$, and the whole group. The group $C_3$ has two subgroups. Because the primary factors have coprime orders, every subgroup is the product of a subgroup from each factor. Hence the Galois group has
$$
5\cdot2=10
$$
subgroups. By the Galois correspondence, $E$ has ten subfields, including $\mathbb Q$ and $E$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $F$ have characteristic zero and not contain a primitive $n$th root of unity. Suppose $X^n-a$ is irreducible over $F$. Show that the Galois group of its splitting field embeds in the affine linear group on $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.

#proof
Let $\alpha^n=a$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $n$th root of unity. The splitting field is
$$
L=F(\alpha,\zeta),
$$
and its roots are $\alpha\zeta^k$, indexed by $k\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.

For $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$, there are $b\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^*$ and $c\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ such that
$$
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^b,
\qquad
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha\zeta^c.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sigma(\alpha\zeta^k)=\alpha\zeta^{bk+c}.
$$
Thus $σ$ acts on the root labels by the affine transformation
$$
k\longmapsto bk+c.
$$
The action on all roots is faithful because they generate $L$, so this gives an embedding of the Galois group into the group of such affine transformations.
::
