::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Let $D_8$ be the dihedral group of $8$ elements.

(a) List all conjugacy classes in $D_8$.

(b) List all irreducible characters of $D_8$.

#proof
Use the presentation
$$
D_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
$$
The conjugacy classes are
$$
\{1\},\quad \{r^2\},\quad \{r,r^3\},\quad
\{s,r^2s\},\quad \{rs,r^3s\}.
$$
Thus there are five irreducible complex characters. The abelianization is $C_2\times C_2$, giving four one-dimensional characters. Together with the two-dimensional geometric representation, the complete character table is
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
&1&r^2&\{r,r^3\}&\{s,r^2s\}&\{rs,r^3s\}\\ \hline
\chi_1&1&1&1&1&1\\
\chi_2&1&1&1&-1&-1\\
\chi_3&1&1&-1&1&-1\\
\chi_4&1&1&-1&-1&1\\
\chi_5&2&-2&0&0&0
\end{array}.
$$
Their squared degrees sum to $4\cdot1^2+2^2=8=|D_8|$, so the list is complete.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Show that every finite field is perfect; that is, every extension of finite fields is separable.

#proof
Let $F$ be a finite field of characteristic $p$. The Frobenius map
$$
\varphi:F\to F,\qquad a\mapsto a^p,
$$
is injective because $a^p=b^p$ implies $(a-b)^p=0$, hence $a=b$. Since $F$ is finite, $\varphi$ is therefore surjective.

An irreducible polynomial over a field of characteristic $p$ can be inseparable only if its derivative is zero, in which case it has the form
$$
f(x)=\sum_j a_jx^{pj}.
$$
Because Frobenius is surjective, write each $a_j=b_j^p$. Then
$$
f(x)=\left(\sum_j b_jx^j\right)^p,
$$
contradicting irreducibility unless $f$ has degree $1$. Thus every irreducible polynomial over $F$ is separable, so $F$ is perfect and every finite extension of finite fields is separable.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let $p$ be an odd prime.

(a) Show that $\mathbb Q(e^{2\pi i/p})$ contains a unique quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q$.

(b) Find a field $F$ such that $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$. Prove your answer.

#proof
Put $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$, where $\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}$. Then
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times,
$$
which is cyclic of order $p-1$. A cyclic group of even order has a unique subgroup of index $2$, namely its subgroup of squares. By the Galois correspondence, $K$ therefore has a unique intermediate field of degree $2$ over $\mathbb Q$. More explicitly, it is
$$
\mathbb Q\bigl(\sqrt{(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p}\bigr).
$$

For (b), take the maximal real subfield of the seventh cyclotomic field:
$$
F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1}).
$$
The group $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)/\mathbb Q)$ is cyclic of order $6$, and complex conjugation is its unique subgroup of order $2$. Its fixed field is $F$, so
$$
[F:\mathbb Q]=3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\cong C_6/C_2\cong C_3.
$$
Thus $F/\mathbb Q$ is a cyclic Galois extension of degree $3$.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Prove that no group of order $462$ is simple.

#proof
Let $|G|=462=2\cdot3\cdot7\cdot11$. The number $n_{11}$ of Sylow $11$-subgroups satisfies
$$
n_{11}\equiv1\pmod{11},qquad n_{11}\mid42.
$$
Among the divisors of $42$, only $1$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $11$. Hence $n_{11}=1$. The unique Sylow $11$-subgroup is normal, so $G$ is not simple.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
For each pair of rings below, either prove that they are isomorphic or prove that they are not isomorphic.

(a) $\mathbb Z/24\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$.

(b) $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-1}]$ and $\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2)$.

(c) $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb R[x]/(x^2+x+\pi)$.

#proof
(a) Since $(3,8)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives
$$
\mathbb Z/24\mathbb Z\cong
\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z.
$$

(b) These rings are not isomorphic. The ring $\mathbb Z[i]$ is an integral domain, whereas the class $\overline x$ in $\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2)$ is nonzero and satisfies $\overline x^{,2}=0$. Thus the second ring has a nonzero nilpotent element and is not a domain.

(c) The discriminant of $x^2+x+\pi$ is $1-4\pi<0$, so the polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb R$. Completing the square gives
$$
x^2+x+\pi=\left(x+\frac12\right)^2+\left(\pi-\frac14\right).
$$
If $\alpha$ denotes the class of $x$, then
$$
u=\frac{\alpha+1/2}{\sqrt{\pi-1/4}}
$$
satisfies $u^2=-1$. Hence the map
$$
\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathbb R[x]/(x^2+x+\pi),
\qquad a+bi\longmapsto a+bu,
$$
is an isomorphism of real algebras.

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb Q$, and let $A\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(V)$. Assume that $A^5=I$ and that $Av=v$ implies $v=0$. Show that $4$ divides $n$.

#proof
Since
$$
x^5-1=(x-1)\Phi_5(x),
\qquad
\Phi_5(x)=x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1,
$$
we have $(A-I)\Phi_5(A)=0$. The hypothesis says that $A-I$ is injective, hence invertible because $V$ is finite-dimensional. Therefore
$$
\Phi_5(A)=0.
$$
The polynomial $\Phi_5$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. Consequently $V$ becomes a vector space over the field
$$
\mathbb Q[x]/(\Phi_5)\cong\mathbb Q(\zeta_5),
$$
with $x$ acting as $A$. This field has degree $4$ over $\mathbb Q$. If $d$ is the dimension of $V$ over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$, then
$$
n=\dim_{\mathbb Q}V=4d.
$$
Thus $4\mid n$.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $T$ be a nilpotent linear operator on an $n$-dimensional vector space $V$ over a field $F$. Show that $T^n=0$.

#proof
Consider the descending chain
$$
V\supseteq T(V)\supseteq T^2(V)\supseteq\cdots.
$$
If $T^j(V)\ne0$ and $T^{j+1}(V)=T^j(V)$, then applying $T$ repeatedly shows
$$
T^{j+k}(V)=T^j(V)\ne0
$$
for every $k\ge0$, contradicting nilpotence. Thus, until the chain reaches $0$, every inclusion is strict. Each strict inclusion lowers dimension by at least one. Starting from dimension $n$, after at most $n$ such decreases the space is zero. Hence $T^n(V)=0$, or $T^n=0$.

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Let a finite group $G$ act on a finite set $S$. Let $\mathbb C[S]$ be the complex vector space with basis $S$, and let $\chi$ be the character of the corresponding permutation representation.

(a) Show that for $\sigma\in G$, $\chi(\sigma)$ is the number of fixed points of $\sigma$ in $S$.

(b) Show that $\langle\chi,1_G\rangle$ is the number of $G$-orbits in $S$.

#proof
Relative to the basis $S$, the matrix of $\sigma$ is a permutation matrix. Its diagonal entry indexed by $s\in S$ is $1$ exactly when $\sigma s=s$, and otherwise it is $0$. Therefore its trace is
$$
\chi(\sigma)=|S^\sigma|,
$$
the number of fixed points.

The inner product with the trivial character is
$$
\langle\chi,1_G\rangle
=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi(\sigma)
=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}|S^\sigma|.
$$
By Burnside's orbit-counting lemma, this average equals the number of $G$-orbits in $S$. Equivalently, it is the dimension of the invariant subspace $\mathbb C[S]^G$, whose basis consists of the sums of the elements in each orbit.

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Let $K$ be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q$ of
$$
f(x)=(x^2-2x-1)(x^2-3).
$$
Determine the Galois group $G$ of $f(x)$ and determine all intermediate fields explicitly.

#proof
The roots of the first factor are $1\pm\sqrt2$, and those of the second are $\pm\sqrt3$. Hence
$$
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).
$$
Neither $\sqrt3$ lies in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ nor $\sqrt2$ lies in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, so $[K:\mathbb Q]=4$. Independently changing the signs of $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt3$ gives four automorphisms. Thus
$$
G\cong C_2\times C_2.
$$

The three subgroups of order $2$ fix, respectively,
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),qquad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt3),qquad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt6).
$$
Together with the fields fixed by the trivial subgroup and the whole group, the complete intermediate-field lattice is
$$
\mathbb Q,quad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),quad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt3),quad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt6),quad
\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Classify up to isomorphism all groups of order $6$.

#proof
Let $G$ have order $6=2\cdot3$. By Sylow's theorems, the number $n_3$ of Sylow $3$-subgroups satisfies $n_3\equiv1\pmod3$ and $n_3\mid2$, hence $n_3=1$. Let $P=\langle a\rangle\cong C_3$ be this normal subgroup, and let $Q=\langle b\rangle\cong C_2$ be a Sylow $2$-subgroup. Then
$$
G\cong C_3\rtimes_\varphi C_2.
$$
There are only two homomorphisms $C_2\to\operatorname{Aut}(C_3)\cong C_2$.

If $\varphi$ is trivial, then
$$
G\cong C_3\times C_2\cong C_6.
$$
If $\varphi$ is nontrivial, then $bab^{-1}=a^{-1}$, and
$$
G\cong\langle a,b\mid a^3=b^2=1,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle
\cong S_3.
$$
These groups are not isomorphic because $C_6$ is abelian and $S_3$ is not. Hence these are exactly the two isomorphism classes.
