::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
For $n\geq2$, prove that there are involutions $\sigma,\tau\in S_{2n}$ such that $\sigma\tau$ has order $n$.

#proof
Let $r=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ n)$ and regard the first $n$ letters as $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. Let $u$ be the reflection $j\mapsto-j$. Then

$$
u^2=1,
\qquad
uru=r^{-1},
$$

so $v=ru$ is also an involution and $vu=r$. To ensure both permutations have order exactly $2$ even in the degenerate small case, let $s=(n+1\ n+2)$, which is disjoint from $r,u,v$, and put

$$
\sigma=vs,
\qquad
\tau=us.
$$

Both have order $2$, and

$$
\sigma\tau=vsus=vu=r,
$$

which has order $n$.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Give a nonabelian semidirect product of two abelian groups.

#proof
The symmetric group $S_3$ is

$$
S_3\cong C_3\rtimes C_2,
$$

where the nonidentity element of $C_2$ acts on $C_3$ by inversion. Both factors are abelian. The product is not abelian because, for generators $a$ of $C_3$ and $b$ of $C_2$,

$$
bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\neq a.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let

$$
A=\left\{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\in\mathbb Q(x):g(0)\neq0\right\}.
$$

**(a)** Prove that $A$ is a PID.

**(b)** Prove that $A$ has only one irreducible element up to associates.

#proof
This is the localization of the PID $\mathbb Q[x]$ at the multiplicative set of polynomials not divisible by $x$. Every nonzero polynomial can be written uniquely as

$$
f(x)=x^m u(x),
\qquad
u(0)\neq0.
$$

The element $u(x)$ is a unit in $A$. Thus every nonzero element of $A$ is a unit times $x^m$.

If $I\neq0$ is an ideal, choose an element of $I$ with least exponent $m$ of $x$. Multiplying by a unit gives $x^m\in I$, and every element of $I$ is divisible by $x^m$. Hence $I=(x^m)$, proving **(a)**.

An element $ux^m$ is irreducible exactly when $m=1$. Therefore every irreducible is associated to $x$, proving **(b)**.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Let $I=(p,f(x))\triangleleft\mathbb Z[x]$, where $p$ is prime and $f$ is nonconstant. Prove that $I$ is maximal if and only if $f$ is irreducible modulo $p$.

#proof
Successive quotienting gives

$$
\mathbb Z[x]/(p,f)
\cong
\mathbb F_p[x]/(\overline f).
$$

The ideal $I$ is maximal exactly when this quotient is a field. Since $\mathbb F_p[x]$ is a PID, $\mathbb F_p[x]/(\overline f)$ is a field exactly when $\overline f$ is irreducible. This proves the equivalence.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Let $K$ have characteristic $5$. For which $n\geq1$ is

$$
x^n-x
$$

separable?

#proof
Let $f=x^n-x$. Its derivative is

$$
f'=nx^{n-1}-1.
$$

If $5\mid n$, then $f'=-1$, so $f$ is separable. Suppose $5\nmid n$. A common root $\alpha$ of $f$ and $f'$ cannot be zero. From $f(\alpha)=0$ we get $\alpha^{n-1}=1$, while $f'(\alpha)=0$ gives

$$
n\alpha^{n-1}=1.
$$

These equations are compatible exactly when $n\equiv1\pmod5$. Hence, for $n>1$, the polynomial is separable exactly when

$$
n\not\equiv1\pmod5.
$$

For $n=1$, the polynomial is zero and is not separable. Thus the complete answer is: all $n\geq2$ with $n\not\equiv1\pmod5$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
For which prime powers $q$ is the additive group of $\mathbb F_q$ cyclic?

#proof
Write $q=p^r$. The additive group is an $r$-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb F_p$, so

$$
(\mathbb F_q,+)\cong(C_p)^r.
$$

This group is cyclic exactly when $r=1$. Therefore the additive group is cyclic precisely when $q$ is prime.

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $F/E$ be a field extension of degree $5$, and let $K$ be the smallest field containing $F$ that is Galois over $E$. Prove that

$$
[K:E]\leq120.
$$

#proof
Because such a finite Galois closure exists, $F/E$ is separable. There are at most five $E$-embeddings of $F$ into an algebraic closure. The Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ acts faithfully on the five conjugate embeddings, and therefore embeds in $S_5$. Hence

$$
[K:E]=|\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)|\leq|S_5|=120.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Give an injective homomorphism of abelian groups $M_1\to M_2$ and an abelian group $N$ such that

$$
M_1\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N\longrightarrow M_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N
$$

is not injective.

#proof
Take the inclusion

$$
2\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z
$$

and let $N=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Identifying $2\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z$, the inclusion corresponds to multiplication by $2$. After tensoring with $N$, the induced map is

$$
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
$$

which is the zero map. Its domain is nonzero, so it is not injective.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
For $A\in M_n(\mathbb R)$, prove that the following are equivalent:

**(a)** The only complex eigenvalue of $A$ is $0$.

**(b)** $A^m=0$ for some $m\geq1$.

**(c)** $A^n=0$.

#proof
If **(a)** holds, the characteristic polynomial is $x^n$. Cayley-Hamilton gives $A^n=0$, so **(a)** implies **(c)**. Clearly **(c)** implies **(b)**. Finally, if $A^m=0$ and $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq0$, then

$$
0=A^mv=\lambda^m v,
$$

so $\lambda=0$. Thus **(b)** implies **(a)**.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $T$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb Q$. If its characteristic polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, prove that its matrix is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$.

#proof
By Cayley-Hamilton, the minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial. Since the characteristic polynomial is irreducible and nonconstant, the minimal polynomial must equal it. Every irreducible polynomial over a characteristic-zero field is separable, so this polynomial has distinct roots in $\mathbb C$. Hence the minimal polynomial of $T$ splits over $\mathbb C$ into distinct linear factors. This is exactly the criterion for diagonalizability over $\mathbb C$.
::
