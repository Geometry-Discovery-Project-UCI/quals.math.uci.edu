::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Give examples, with justification, of:

(a) two nonisomorphic rings with isomorphic additive groups;

(b) a prime ideal that is not maximal in an integral domain;

(c) subgroups $H\subseteq K\subseteq G$ with $K\trianglelefteq G$ and $H\trianglelefteq K$, but $H\not\trianglelefteq G$.

#proof
(a) Take $\mathbb F_4$ and $\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2$. Their additive groups are both $C_2\times C_2$, but the first ring is a field and the second has zero divisors.

(b) In $R=\mathbb Z$, the ideal $(0)$ is prime because $\mathbb Z$ is a domain, but it is not maximal because $\mathbb Z$ is not a field.

(c) Let $G=A_4$, let $K=V_4$ be the normal Klein four subgroup, and let
$$
H=\langle(12)(34)\rangle.
$$
Since $K$ is abelian, $H\trianglelefteq K$. But conjugation by a $3$-cycle permutes the three subgroups of order $2$ in $V_4$, so $H$ is not normal in $A_4$.

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Count all subgroups, including the trivial group and the whole group, of:

(a) a cyclic group of order $63$;

(b) the additive group $\mathbb F_{17}^3$;

(c) the dihedral group $D_8$ of order $8$.

#proof
(a) A cyclic group has one subgroup for each divisor of its order. Since
$$
63=3^2\cdot7,
$$
the number of divisors is
$$
(2+1)(1+1)=6.
$$

(b) Subgroups are exactly vector subspaces. There is one subspace each of dimensions $0$ and $3$. The number of lines is
$$
\frac{17^3-1}{17-1}=17^2+17+1=307,
$$
and by duality the number of planes is also $307$. Thus the total is
$$
1+307+307+1=616.
$$

(c) Write $D_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1, srs=r^{-1}\rangle$. Besides $1$ and $D_8$, there are five subgroups of order $2$,
$$
\langle r^2\rangle,\ \langle s\rangle,\ \langle rs\rangle,
\ \langle r^2s\rangle,\ \langle r^3s\rangle,
$$
and three of order $4$,
$$
\langle r\rangle,
\quad\langle r^2,s\rangle,
\quad\langle r^2,rs\rangle.
$$
Hence there are $10$ subgroups.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Prove that there are no simple groups of order $30$.

#proof
The number of Sylow $5$-subgroups is $1$ or $6$, and the number of Sylow $3$-subgroups is $1$ or $10$. If either is $1$, that Sylow subgroup is normal.

Otherwise, the Sylow $5$-subgroups contribute $6(5-1)=24$ nonidentity elements and the Sylow $3$-subgroups contribute $10(3-1)=20$ different nonidentity elements. These disjoint sets cannot fit among the $29$ nonidentity elements of $G$. Hence some Sylow subgroup is normal, and $G$ is not simple.

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Determine the splitting field and Galois group of $x^4-3$ over $\mathbb Q$. Give the subgroup and subfield lattices, with fixed fields identified.

#proof
Let $a=\sqrt[4]{3}$. The roots are $a,ia,-a,-ia$, so the splitting field is
$$
L=\mathbb Q(a,i).
$$
Eisenstein's criterion at $3$ gives $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q]=4$, and $i$ is not in this real field. Hence $[L:\mathbb Q]=8$.

Define
$$
r(a)=ia,\quad r(i)=i,
\qquad s(a)=a,\quad s(i)=-i.
$$
Then $r^4=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$, so
$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\cong D_8.
$$
The full fixed-field correspondence is
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
H&L^H\\ \hline
G&\mathbb Q\\
\langle r\rangle&\mathbb Q(i)\\
\langle r^2,s\rangle&\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\\
\langle r^2,rs\rangle&\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\\
\langle r^2\rangle&\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,i)\\
\langle s\rangle&\mathbb Q(a)\\
\langle r^2s\rangle&\mathbb Q(ia)\\
\langle rs\rangle&\mathbb Q((1+i)a)\\
\langle r^3s\rangle&\mathbb Q((1-i)a)\\
\{1\}&L
\end{array}
$$
The three order-$4$ subgroups are $\langle r\rangle$, $\langle r^2,s\rangle$, and $\langle r^2,rs\rangle$. The first contains $\langle r^2\rangle$; the second contains $\langle r^2\rangle,\langle s\rangle,\langle r^2s\rangle$; and the third contains $\langle r^2\rangle,\langle rs\rangle,\langle r^3s\rangle$. The subfield lattice reverses these inclusions.

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Let $G_1,G_2$ be finite groups of relatively prime orders.

(a) Prove that every subgroup $H\le G_1\times G_2$ is $H_1\times H_2$ for subgroups $H_i\le G_i$.

(b) Give a counterexample without the coprime-order assumption.

#proof
(a) Let $H_i$ be the image of $H$ under projection to $G_i$. If $(g_1,g_2)\in H$, the orders of $g_1$ and $g_2$ are relatively prime. By the Chinese remainder theorem, there is an integer $a$ such that
$$
a\equiv1\pmod{|g_1|},
\qquad a\equiv0\pmod{|g_2|}.
$$
Then
$$
(g_1,g_2)^a=(g_1,1)\in H.
$$
Similarly $(1,g_2)\in H$. Thus $H$ contains $H_1\times H_2$, while the reverse inclusion follows from the definition of the projections. Hence $H=H_1\times H_2$.

(b) In $C_2\times C_2$, the diagonal subgroup
$$
\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
$$
is not a product of a subgroup of the first factor and a subgroup of the second.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Suppose $G$ is finite and a nontrivial subgroup $H$ is contained in every nontrivial subgroup of $G$.

(a) Prove that $|G|$ is a power of a prime $p$ and that $G$ has exactly $p-1$ elements of order $p$.

(b) Give such an example with $G$ nonabelian of order $8$.

#proof
(a) If two different primes $p,q$ divided $|G|$, Cauchy's theorem would give subgroups of orders $p$ and $q$. Both would contain $H$, forcing $|H|$ to divide both $p$ and $q$, impossible because $H$ is nontrivial. Hence $|G|$ is a power of one prime $p$.

Every subgroup of order $p$ contains $H$. Since such a subgroup has no nontrivial proper subgroup, it follows that $H$ equals every subgroup of order $p$. Thus there is exactly one such subgroup, and its $p-1$ nonidentity elements are exactly the elements of order $p$.

(b) Take $G=Q_8$ and
$$
H=\{1,-1\}.
$$
Every nontrivial subgroup of $Q_8$ contains $-1$, so it contains $H$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Find all prime ideals of
$$
A=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^3).
$$

#proof
The class $\overline X$ is nilpotent, and every prime ideal contains every nilpotent element. Thus every prime ideal of $A$ contains $(\overline X)$. Prime ideals containing it correspond to prime ideals of
$$
A/(\overline X)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
Therefore the complete list is
$$
(\overline X)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(p,\overline X),
$$
where $p$ ranges over the rational primes.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
Let $L\subset\mathbb C$ be finite Galois over $\mathbb Q$ with cyclic Galois group. Let $K\subseteq L$ and suppose $K\not\subseteq\mathbb R$. Prove that

(a) $[K:\mathbb Q]$ is even;

(b) $[L:K]$ is odd.

#proof
Let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)$ and $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Complex conjugation restricts to an element $c\in G$ of order $2$. Since $K$ is not real, $c$ does not fix $K$ pointwise, so $c\notin H$.

(a) The nontrivial coset $cH$ has order $2$ in the cyclic quotient $G/H$. Hence
$$
[K:\mathbb Q]=|G/H|
$$
is even.

(b) A cyclic group has a unique element of order $2$. If $|H|=[L:K]$ were even, then $H$ would contain that unique involution $c$, contradicting $c\notin H$. Therefore $[L:K]$ is odd.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem
Let $p$ be an odd prime. Show that there are exactly five groups of order $2p^2$ up to isomorphism.

#proof
The Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is unique because its number divides $2$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo $p$. By Schur--Zassenhaus,
$$
G\cong P\rtimes C_2.
$$
There are two possibilities for $P$.

If $P\cong C_{p^2}$, an involutory automorphism is either the identity or inversion. This gives
$$
C_{2p^2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
D_{2p^2}.
$$

If $P\cong C_p\times C_p$, the action is an involution in $GL_2(\mathbb F_p)$. Since $p$ is odd, every such operator is diagonalizable with eigenvalues in $\{1,-1\}$. Up to conjugacy there are three possibilities:
$$
I,qquad -I,qquad\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
$$
They give respectively
$$
C_2\times C_p\times C_p,
$$
$$
(C_p\times C_p)\rtimes_{-I}C_2,
$$
and
$$
C_p\times D_{2p}.
$$
These five groups are pairwise nonisomorphic: the two abelian groups have different exponents, and the three nonabelian groups have Sylow $p$-subgroups or involution actions of different types. Hence the list is complete.

::ProblemBlock{number=10}
#problem
Let $A\in GL_8(\mathbb Q)$ have characteristic polynomial
$$
(x^3+2)^2(x^2-3).
$$

(a) Find all possible minimal polynomials.

(b) Find the trace and determinant.

(c) Count the conjugacy classes and give a representative of each.

#proof
The polynomials
$$
f=x^3+2,
\qquad g=x^2-3
$$
are irreducible and relatively prime over $\mathbb Q$. The $g$-primary part occurs once. The multiplicity-two $f$-primary part has either one block for $f^2$ or two blocks for $f$.

(a) Thus the possible minimal polynomials are
$$
f^2g
\quad\text{and}\quad
fg.
$$

(b) The coefficient of $x^7$ in $f^2g$ is zero, so
$$
\operatorname{tr}A=0.
$$
Since the dimension is even, the determinant equals the constant term of the characteristic polynomial:
$$
\det A=2^2(-3)=-12.
$$

(c) There are exactly two conjugacy classes. If $C(h)$ denotes the companion matrix of $h$, representatives are
$$
C(f^2)\oplus C(g)
$$
and
$$
C(f)\oplus C(f)\oplus C(g).
$$
Their minimal polynomials are $f^2g$ and $fg$, so they are not conjugate.
