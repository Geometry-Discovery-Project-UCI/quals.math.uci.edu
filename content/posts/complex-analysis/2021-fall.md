::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem

Compute
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{5+3\cos\theta}\,d\theta.
$$

#proof

Use the standard formula
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}
=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}},
\qquad a>|b|.
$$
Here $a=5$ and $b=3$, so
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+3\cos\theta}
=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{25-9}}
=\frac{2\pi}{4}
=\frac{\pi}{2}.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem

Let $p(z)$ be a polynomial of degree $d$. Prove that if
$$
\{z\in\mathbb C:p(z)=0\}\subset D(0,1),
$$
then
$$
\{z\in\mathbb C:p'(z)=0\}\subset D(0,1).
$$

#proof

This is the Gauss-Lucas theorem: the zeros of $p'$ lie in the convex hull of the zeros of $p$.

Since all zeros of $p$ lie in the unit disk $D(0,1)$, their convex hull is contained in $D(0,1)$. Therefore all zeros of $p'$ also lie in $D(0,1)$.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem

Let
$$
f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_jz^j
$$
be entire and suppose
$$
|f(z)|\le e^{|z|}
$$
for all $z\in\mathbb C$. Prove that
$$
|c_n|\le \left(\frac en\right)^n,
\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots.
$$

#proof

By Cauchy's estimate, for any $r>0$,
$$
|c_n|\le \frac{\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|}{r^n}
\le \frac{e^r}{r^n}.
$$
Choose $r=n$. Then
$$
|c_n|\le \frac{e^n}{n^n}
=\left(\frac en\right)^n.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem

Let $f:D(0,1)\to D(0,1)$ be holomorphic with
$$
f(0)=3^{-10}e^{i\pi/10}.
$$

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(i)** Prove that $f$ has at most $10$ zeros in $D(0,1/3)$, counting multiplicities.

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(ii)** Provide an example of such $f$ which has $10$ zeros in $D(0,1/3)$, counting multiplicities.

#proof

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(i)** Let $a_1,\ldots,a_N$ be the zeros of $f$ in $D(0,1/3)$, counted with multiplicity. Jensen's formula, together with $|f|\le1$, gives
$$
|f(0)|\le \prod_{j=1}^{N}|a_j|.
$$
Since $|a_j|<1/3$,
$$
\prod_{j=1}^{N}|a_j|<3^{-N}.
$$
But
$$
|f(0)|=3^{-10}.
$$
Therefore
$$
3^{-10}<3^{-N},
$$
so
$$
N<10.
$$
In particular, $N\le10$.

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(ii)** With the usual convention that $D(0,1/3)$ is open, the sharp bound is not attained. Indeed, the argument above gives $N<10$, so one cannot have exactly $10$ zeros strictly inside $D(0,1/3)$.

If the intended disk in part **(ii)** is the closed disk $|z|\le1/3$, then an extremal example is the finite Blaschke product
$$
f(z)=e^{i\pi/10}\left(\frac{z-1/3}{1-z/3}\right)^{10}.
$$
It maps $D(0,1)$ to itself, has $10$ zeros at $z=1/3$, and satisfies
$$
f(0)=e^{i\pi/10}3^{-10}.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem

Prove or disprove: there is a holomorphic function $f$ in
$$
D=\mathbb C\setminus D(0,4)
$$
such that
$$
f'(z)=
\frac{\cos(\pi z)}{z-1}
\frac{z}{z-2}
\frac{e^{3-z}}{(z-3)^2}.
$$

#proof

Let
$$
g(z)=
\frac{\cos(\pi z)}{z-1}
\frac{z}{z-2}
\frac{e^{3-z}}{(z-3)^2}.
$$
The function $g$ is holomorphic on the exterior domain $\mathbb C\setminus D(0,4)$. A primitive exists on this domain exactly when the period around the hole is zero, equivalently when the sum of residues at the enclosed poles is zero.

The residues are
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}g=\cos\pi=-1,
$$
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=2}g=2,
$$
and
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=3}g
=\left.\frac{d}{dz}e^{3-z}\right|_{z=3}
=-1.
$$
Their sum is
$$
-1+2-1=0.
$$
Therefore the period around the hole is zero, and $g$ has a holomorphic primitive on $D$. Hence such an $f$ exists.

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem

Find a conformal map from
$$
D=\{z=x+iy:0<x<1,\ y>0\}
$$
onto the unit disk $D(0,1)$.

#proof

First set
$$
w=e^{\pi iz}.
$$
Since $0<x<1$ and $y>0$, this maps $D$ onto the upper half of the unit disk:
$$
\{w:|w|<1,\ \operatorname{Im}w>0\}.
$$
Next define
$$
\eta=\frac{1+w}{1-w}.
$$
This maps the upper half-disk onto the first quadrant. Squaring maps the first quadrant onto the upper half-plane:
$$
\xi=\eta^2.
$$
Finally, the Cayley map
$$
\Phi(\xi)=\frac{\xi-i}{\xi+i}
$$
maps the upper half-plane onto the unit disk.

Thus one conformal map is
$$

\Phi(z)=
\frac{
\left(\frac{1+e^{\pi iz}}{1-e^{\pi iz}}\right)^2-i
}{
\left(\frac{1+e^{\pi iz}}{1-e^{\pi iz}}\right)^2+i
}.

$$

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem

Prove that all zeros of
$$
f(z)=z^8+4z^3-5
$$
lie in the annulus
$$
D(0,2)\setminus D(0,1).
$$

#proof

If $|z|<1$, then
$$
|z^8+4z^3|<1+4=5.
$$
Thus
$$
z^8+4z^3-5\ne0
$$
for $|z|<1$.

If $|z|\ge2$, then
$$
|z|^8>4|z|^3+5.
$$
Therefore
$$
|z^8|>|4z^3-5|,
$$
so
$$
z^8+4z^3-5\ne0
$$
for $|z|\ge2$.

Hence every zero satisfies
$$
1\le |z|<2,
$$
which is exactly $D(0,2)\setminus D(0,1)$ under the usual open-disk convention.

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem

Find all entire holomorphic functions $f$ satisfying
$$
\left|f\left(\frac{1}{\log(n+1)}\right)\right|\le \frac1n
$$
for all positive integers $n$.

#proof

The points
$$
z_n=\frac1{\log(n+1)}
$$
tend to $0$. The estimate gives
$$
f(z_n)\to0,
$$
so by continuity,
$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Suppose $f$ is not identically zero. Then $f$ has a zero of finite order $m\ge1$ at $0$, so
$$
f(z)=cz^m+O(z^{m+1}),
\qquad c\ne0.
$$
Thus, for large $n$,
$$
|f(z_n)|\ge \frac{|c|}{2}\frac1{(\log(n+1))^m}.
$$
But
$$
\frac1{(\log(n+1))^m}
$$
is eventually much larger than $1/n$, contradicting the hypothesis.

Therefore $f$ must vanish to infinite order at $0$, hence
$$
f\equiv0.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem

Let $f$ be holomorphic in $D(0,1)$ and satisfy
$$
|f(z)|\le1
$$
on $D(0,1)$. Prove that
$$
\frac{|f(0)|-|z|}{1+|f(0)||z|}
\le
|f(z)|
\le
\frac{|f(0)|+|z|}{1-|f(0)||z|},
\qquad z\in D(0,1).
$$

#proof

Let
$$
a=f(0).
$$
If $|a|=1$, then by the maximum modulus principle $f$ is constant, and the result is immediate. Assume $|a|<1$.

Apply Schwarz-Pick to $f$:
$$
\left|\frac{f(z)-a}{1-\overline a f(z)}\right|\le |z|.
$$
Let $r=|z|$ and $w=f(z)$. The inequality says that the pseudo-hyperbolic distance from $w$ to $a$ is at most $r$. The corresponding Euclidean bounds on $|w|$ are
$$
\frac{|a|-r}{1+|a|r}
\le |w|\le
\frac{|a|+r}{1-|a|r}.
$$
Substituting back $w=f(z)$ and $a=f(0)$ gives the desired inequalities.
::
