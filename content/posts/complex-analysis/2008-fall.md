# 2008 Fall Qualifying Exam in Complex Analysis

::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem
Compute the area of the image of the unit disk $D=\{z:|z|<1\}$ under
$$
f(z)=z+\frac{z^2}{2}.
$$

#proof
The map is univalent on the unit disk; indeed $f'(z)=1+z$ has no zero in $|z|<1$. Hence the area of the image is
$$
\int_D |f'(z)|^2\,dA(z).
$$
Since
$$
f'(z)=1+z,
$$
we get
$$
\int_D |1+z|^2\,dA
=\int_D (1+z+\overline z+|z|^2)\,dA.
$$
The integrals of $z$ and $\overline z$ over the disk are $0$, so
$$
\operatorname{Area}(f(D))
=\int_D 1\,dA+\int_D |z|^2\,dA
=\pi+2\pi\int_0^1 r^3\,dr
=\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}
=\frac{3\pi}{2}.
$$
Thus the area is
$$
\frac{3\pi}{2}.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem
Find all entire functions $f(z)$ satisfying
$$
f''\left(\frac1n\right)=4f\left(\frac1n\right)
$$
for all $n\in\mathbb N$.

#proof
The function
$$
g(z)=f''(z)-4f(z)
$$
is entire and vanishes at $z=1/n$ for every $n$. Since these points accumulate at $0$, the identity theorem gives
$$
g\equiv 0.
$$
Thus
$$
f''=4f.
$$
The entire solutions of this differential equation are
$$
f(z)=Ae^{2z}+Be^{-2z},\qquad A,B\in\mathbb C.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem
Let
$$
L=\{z=x+iy:x=y\}.
$$
Assume $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ is entire and maps $L$ into $L$. If
$$
f(1)=0,
$$
prove that
$$
f(i)=0.
$$

#proof
Reflection across the line $L$ is
$$
\sigma(z)=i\overline z.
$$
Since $f$ maps $L$ into itself, the Schwarz reflection principle gives
$$
f(\sigma(z))=\sigma(f(z)).
$$
Taking $z=1$,
$$
\sigma(1)=i.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(i)=f(\sigma(1))=\sigma(f(1))=\sigma(0)=0.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem
Find the largest disk centered at $1$ in which the Taylor series
$$
\frac{1}{1+z^2}=\sum a_k(z-1)^k
$$
converges.

#proof
The function
$$
\frac{1}{1+z^2}
$$
has singularities at
$$
z=i
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z=-i.
$$
The radius of convergence of the Taylor series centered at $1$ is the distance from $1$ to the nearest singularity.

Both distances are
$$
|1-i|=|1+i|=\sqrt2.
$$
Thus the largest disk is
$$
D(1,\sqrt2).
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem
Evaluate
$$
\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\,dx.
$$

#proof
Use the standard identity
$$
\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin ax}{x}\right)^2\,dx=\frac{\pi a}{2},
\qquad a>0.
$$
Taking $a=1$ gives
$$
\displaystyle
\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem
Suppose $f:D\to D$, where $D=\{|z|<1\}$, is holomorphic and
$$
f(0)=\alpha\neq 0.
$$
Show that $f$ cannot have a zero in the disk
$$
D(0,|\alpha|)=\{z:|z|<|\alpha|\}.
$$

#proof
Suppose $f(\beta)=0$. By the Schwarz-Pick lemma,
$$
\left|\frac{f(\beta)-f(0)}{1-\overline{f(0)}f(\beta)}\right|
\leq
\left|\frac{\beta-0}{1-\overline 0\beta}\right|.
$$
Since $f(0)=\alpha$ and $f(\beta)=0$, this becomes
$$
|\alpha|\leq |\beta|.
$$
Therefore every zero $\beta$ of $f$ satisfies $|\beta|\geq |\alpha|$. Hence $f$ has no zero in $D(0,|\alpha|)$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem
Let $u$ be harmonic on $\mathbb R^2$ and suppose $u(x)\neq 0$ for all $x\in\mathbb R^2$. Show that $u$ is constant.

#proof
Since $u$ is continuous and never zero on the connected set $\mathbb R^2$, it has one fixed sign. Thus either $u>0$ everywhere or $u<0$ everywhere.

A harmonic function on the whole plane that is bounded below or bounded above is constant. Indeed, apply Harnack's inequality to the positive harmonic function $u$ or $-u$ and let the radius tend to infinity.

Therefore $u$ is constant.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem
How many zeros does
$$
f(z)=14z^{100}-5e^z
$$
have in the unit disk? What are the multiplicities of the zeros?

#proof
On $|z|=1$,
$$
|5e^z|=5e^{\operatorname{Re}z}\leq 5e<14=|14z^{100}|.
$$
By Rouche's theorem, $14z^{100}-5e^z$ and $14z^{100}$ have the same number of zeros in the unit disk, counted with multiplicity. Hence $f$ has $100$ zeros in the unit disk.

They are all simple. If $z$ were a multiple zero, then
$$
14z^{100}=5e^z
$$
and
$$
1400z^{99}=5e^z.
$$
Dividing the second equation by the first gives
$$
\frac{100}{z}=1,
$$
so $z=100$, which is not in the unit disk. Therefore no zero in the unit disk is multiple.

Thus $f$ has
$$
100
$$
zeros in the unit disk, all of multiplicity $1$.
::
