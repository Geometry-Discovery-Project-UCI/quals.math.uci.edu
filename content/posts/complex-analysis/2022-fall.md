# 2022 Fall Qualifying Exam in Complex Analysis

::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem

Let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, and let $f:D\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic such that
$$
|\operatorname{Re}f(z)|<1
$$
for all $z\in D$. Show that
$$
|f'(0)|\le2.
$$

#proof

Let
$$
u(z)=\operatorname{Re}f(z).
$$
Then $u$ is harmonic on $D$ and satisfies
$$
|u(z)|<1.
$$
The sharp gradient estimate for bounded harmonic functions on the unit disk gives
$$
|\nabla u(0)|\le \frac4\pi.
$$
Since $f=u+iv$ is holomorphic, the Cauchy-Riemann equations imply
$$
|f'(0)|=|\nabla u(0)|.
$$
Therefore
$$
|f'(0)|\le \frac4\pi<2.
$$
In particular,
$$
|f'(0)|\le2.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem

Let $f$ be an entire function. Show that
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(z)}{n^n}
$$
converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb C$.

#proof

Let $K\subset\mathbb C$ be compact. Let
$$
K_1=\{w\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{dist}(w,K)\le1\}.
$$
Since $f$ is entire, it is bounded on $K_1$; say
$$
|f(w)|\le M
$$
for $w\in K_1$.

For every $z\in K$, Cauchy's estimate on the circle $|\zeta-z|=1$ gives
$$
|f^{(n)}(z)|\le n!M.
$$
Therefore
$$
\left|\frac{f^{(n)}(z)}{n^n}\right|
\le M\frac{n!}{n^n}.
$$
By Stirling's formula,
$$
\frac{n!}{n^n}\sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\,e^{-n},
$$
so
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{n^n}
$$
converges. The Weierstrass $M$-test now gives uniform convergence on $K$.

Since $K$ was arbitrary, the series converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb C$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem

Let $f$ be an entire function. Suppose the family
$$
F=\{f_n:f_n(z)=f(nz)\}
$$
is a normal family on the annulus
$$
\{1<|z|<2\}.
$$
Show that $f$ is constant.

#proof

For holomorphic functions with values in $\mathbb C$, normality implies local boundedness. Hence the family
$$
\{f(nz)\}
$$
is locally bounded on the annulus $1<|z|<2$.

Choose the compact subannulus
$$
K=\{z:1.1\le |z|\le 1.9\}.
$$
Then there exists $M>0$ such that
$$
|f(nz)|\le M
$$
for all $n$ and all $z\in K$.

The sets
$$
nK=\{w:1.1n\le |w|\le1.9n\}
$$
cover all sufficiently large $|w|$, because the intervals $[1.1n,1.9n]$ overlap for large $n$. Therefore $f$ is bounded outside a sufficiently large disk. Since $f$ is entire, it is also bounded on the remaining compact disk. Thus $f$ is bounded on all of $\mathbb C$.

By Liouville's theorem, $f$ is constant.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem

Let $p,q$ be polynomials on $\mathbb C$ and assume that
$$
p(0)=0,\qquad p'(0)\ne0,
$$
and
$$
|p(z)|>0
$$
for all $0<|z|\le1$.

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(i)** Show that there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $\varepsilon\in\mathbb C$ with $|\varepsilon|<\delta$, the polynomial
$$
z\mapsto p(z)+\varepsilon q(z)
$$
has a unique root $z(\varepsilon)\in D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, and this root is simple.

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(ii)** Prove that $\varepsilon\mapsto z(\varepsilon)$ is holomorphic on $|\varepsilon|<\delta$.

#proof

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(i)** Since $p$ has no zeros on $0<|z|\le1$ and $p(0)=0$, the only zero of $p$ in $\overline D$ is $0$. Since $p'(0)\ne0$, this zero is simple.

On $|z|=1$, let
$$
m=\min_{|z|=1}|p(z)|>0.
$$
Let
$$
M=\max_{|z|=1}|q(z)|.
$$
Choose $\delta>0$ such that
$$
\delta M<m.
$$
Then for $|\varepsilon|<\delta$ and $|z|=1$,
$$
|\varepsilon q(z)|<|p(z)|.
$$
By Rouche's theorem, $p$ and $p+\varepsilon q$ have the same number of zeros in $D$, counted with multiplicity. Since $p$ has exactly one zero in $D$, and it is simple, $p+\varepsilon q$ has exactly one zero in $D$, counted with multiplicity. Therefore this zero is unique and simple.

<span style="display:inline-block; width:1em;"></span> **(ii)** Define
$$
F(z,\varepsilon)=p(z)+\varepsilon q(z).
$$
At the zero $z(\varepsilon)$, we have
$$
F(z(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=0.
$$
The zero is simple, so
$$
\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}(z(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\ne0.
$$
By the holomorphic implicit function theorem, $z(\varepsilon)$ depends holomorphically on $\varepsilon$ for $|\varepsilon|<\delta$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem

Let $\mathbb C_+=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$. Determine all holomorphic functions $f:\mathbb C_+\to\mathbb C$ that satisfy
$$
f(i\sqrt n)=n
$$
and
$$
|f^{(n)}(i)|\le3
$$
for $n=1,2,\ldots$.

#proof

The function
$$
f(z)=-z^2
$$
clearly works, because
$$
f(i\sqrt n)=-(i\sqrt n)^2=n,
$$
and
$$
f'(i)=-2i,\qquad f''(i)=-2,\qquad f^{(n)}(i)=0\quad(n\ge3),
$$
so all the required derivative bounds hold.

We prove it is the only solution. The derivative bounds imply that the Taylor series of $f$ at $i$,
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(i)}{n!}(z-i)^n,
$$
has infinite radius of convergence. Hence it defines an entire function agreeing with $f$ near $i$, and therefore agreeing with $f$ on the whole upper half-plane by analytic continuation.

Let
$$
h(z)=f(z)+z^2.
$$
Then $h$ is entire and
$$
h(i\sqrt n)=0
$$
for all $n\in\mathbb N$.

The derivative bounds at $i$ show that $f$ has growth at most exponential of type $1$:
$$
|f(z)|\le C e^{|z-i|}.
$$
Hence $h$ also has growth at most exponential of order $1$.

But the zeros $i\sqrt n$ have counting function comparable to $R^2$ in the disk $|z|\le R$. Jensen's formula would then force an entire function of exponential type $1$ with these zeros to grow at least like $e^{cR^2}$, unless it is identically zero. Therefore
$$
h\equiv0.
$$
Thus
$$
f(z)=-z^2.
$$
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem

Let $D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$. Let $f$ be holomorphic in a neighborhood of $\overline D$ and suppose
$$
|f(0)|+|f'(0)|
<
\inf\{|f(z)|:|z|=1\}.
$$
Show that $f$ has at least two zeros, counting multiplicity, in $D$.

#proof

Let
$$
g(z)=f(0)+f'(0)z.
$$
On $|z|=1$,
$$
|g(z)|\le |f(0)|+|f'(0)|<|f(z)|.
$$
By Rouche's theorem, $f$ and
$$
f-g
$$
have the same number of zeros in $D$, counted with multiplicity.

But
$$
(f-g)(0)=0
$$
and
$$
(f-g)'(0)=0.
$$
Thus $f-g$ has a zero of order at least $2$ at $0$. Therefore $f$ has at least two zeros in $D$, counting multiplicity.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C$ be open, bounded, and simply connected, and let $f:\Omega\to\Omega$ be holomorphic such that
$$
f(0)=0,\qquad |f'(0)|<1.
$$
Let
$$
f^{(n)}=f\circ f\circ\cdots\circ f
$$
be the $n$-fold composition. Show that
$$
f^{(n)}\to0
$$
uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$.

#proof

Since $\Omega$ is bounded and simply connected and contains $0$, let
$$
\phi:D\to\Omega
$$
be a Riemann map with $\phi(0)=0$. Define
$$
g=\phi^{-1}\circ f\circ \phi.
$$
Then $g:D\to D$ is holomorphic,
$$
g(0)=0,
$$
and
$$
|g'(0)|=|f'(0)|<1.
$$

By Schwarz's lemma,
$$
|g(z)|\le |z|.
$$
Since $|g'(0)|<1$, $g$ is not a rotation. Therefore
$$
|g(z)|<|z|
$$
for every $z\ne0$.

Fix $0<r<1$. By compactness,
$$
c_r=\sup_{|z|\le r,\ z\ne0}\frac{|g(z)|}{|z|}<1.
$$
Hence
$$
|g^{(n)}(z)|\le c_r^n |z|
$$
for $|z|\le r$. Thus $g^{(n)}\to0$ uniformly on compact subsets of $D$.

Conjugating back by $\phi$, we get
$$
f^{(n)}\to0
$$
uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem

Suppose $f$ and $g$ are entire functions with no common zeros. Show that there exist entire functions $F$ and $G$ such that
$$
fF+gG=1.
$$

#proof

We construct $F$ first. Let $\{a_j\}$ be the zeros of $g$, with multiplicities $m_j$. Since $f$ and $g$ have no common zeros,
$$
f(a_j)\ne0
$$
for every $j$.

At each zero $a_j$ of $g$, prescribe the Taylor polynomial of $F$ up to order $m_j-1$ so that
$$
fF\equiv1\pmod{(z-a_j)^{m_j}}.
$$
Equivalently, $F$ should match the Taylor expansion of $1/f$ at $a_j$ through order $m_j-1$.

By the Mittag-Leffler interpolation theorem, there exists an entire function $F$ satisfying all these prescribed finite jet conditions at the discrete set of zeros of $g$.

Then
$$
1-fF
$$
vanishes at each zero $a_j$ of $g$ to order at least $m_j$. Therefore
$$
G=\frac{1-fF}{g}
$$
has removable singularities at the zeros of $g$ and is entire. Hence
$$
fF+gG=1.
$$
::
