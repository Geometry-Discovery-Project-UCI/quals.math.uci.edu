::ProblemBlock{number=1}
#problem

Let $f:H\to H$ be a holomorphic map from the upper half-plane to itself. Show that for all $z\in H$,
$$
|f'(z)|\le \frac{\operatorname{Im}f(z)}{\operatorname{Im}z}.
$$

#proof

This is the Schwarz-Pick lemma for the upper half-plane.

Let
$$
\phi_z(w)=\frac{w-z}{w-\overline z}
$$
be the automorphism from $H$ to $D$ sending $z$ to $0$. Similarly, let
$$
\phi_{f(z)}(w)=\frac{w-f(z)}{w-\overline{f(z)}}.
$$
Then
$$
g=\phi_{f(z)}\circ f\circ \phi_z^{-1}
$$
is a holomorphic map from $D$ to $D$ with $g(0)=0$. By Schwarz's lemma,
$$
|g'(0)|\le1.
$$
Computing the derivative gives
$$
|g'(0)|
=|f'(z)|\frac{\operatorname{Im}z}{\operatorname{Im}f(z)}.
$$
Therefore
$$

|f'(z)|\le \frac{\operatorname{Im}f(z)}{\operatorname{Im}z}.

$$

::ProblemBlock{number=2}
#problem

Let $f$ be a non-constant entire function. Prove that the image $f(\mathbb C)$ is dense in $\mathbb C$. Do not use Picard's theorem.

#proof

Suppose $f(\mathbb C)$ is not dense in $\mathbb C$. Then there exist $a\in\mathbb C$ and $\rho>0$ such that
$$
|f(z)-a|\ge\rho
$$
for all $z\in\mathbb C$.

Then
$$
g(z)=\frac{1}{f(z)-a}
$$
is an entire function satisfying
$$
|g(z)|\le \frac1\rho
$$
on $\mathbb C$. By Liouville's theorem, $g$ is constant. Therefore $f$ is constant, contradicting the hypothesis.

Hence $f(\mathbb C)$ is dense in $\mathbb C$.

::ProblemBlock{number=3}
#problem

Let $f(z)$ be an entire function with $f(0)=1$. For $r>0$, let $n(r)$ denote the number of zeros of $f$ in the closed disk $|z|\le r$. Let
$$
M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.
$$
Prove that for any $r>0$,
$$
\int_0^r \frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt\le \log M(2r).
$$

#proof

Since $f(0)=1$, zero is not a zero of $f$. Jensen's formula says that for $R>0$,
$$
\int_0^R \frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(Re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta-\log|f(0)|.
$$
Since $f(0)=1$, this becomes
$$
\int_0^R \frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(Re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_0^R \frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt
\le \log M(R).
$$
Taking $R=2r$, we get
$$
\int_0^{2r}\frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt\le \log M(2r).
$$
Since the integrand is nonnegative,
$$
\int_0^r\frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt
\le
\int_0^{2r}\frac{n(t)}{t}\,dt
\le \log M(2r).
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=4}
#problem

Prove that for every entire function $f(z)$,
$$
\max_{|z|=1}\left|z-f\left(\frac1z\right)\right|\ge1.
$$

#proof

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
$$
\left|z-f\left(\frac1z\right)\right|<1
$$
for every $|z|=1$.

Put $w=1/z$. On $|z|=1$, we also have $|w|=1$, and the inequality becomes
$$
\left|\frac1w-f(w)\right|<1.
$$
Multiplying by $|w|=1$ gives
$$
|1-wf(w)|<1
$$
on $|w|=1$.

Let
$$
g(w)=wf(w).
$$
Then $g$ is holomorphic on the unit disk and $g(0)=0$. On $|w|=1$,
$$
|g(w)-1|<1.
$$
By Rouche's theorem, $g$ and the constant function $1$ have the same number of zeros in $D(0,1)$. The function $1$ has no zeros, while $g(0)=0$. This contradiction proves
$$
\max_{|z|=1}\left|z-f\left(\frac1z\right)\right|\ge1.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=5}
#problem

Let $a\in\mathbb C$ with $|a|>1$. Determine all meromorphic functions $f$ on $\mathbb C$ whose only singular points are simple poles at $a^n$ for $n=1,2,3,\ldots$, with
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=a^n}f(z)=a^{-n}.
$$
Repeat the problem with the residue condition replaced by
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=a^n}f(z)=a^n.
$$

#proof

First suppose
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=a^n}f(z)=a^{-n}.
$$
The series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{-n}}{z-a^n}
$$
converges normally on compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\{a^n:n\ge1\}$, since for fixed compact sets the terms are $O(a^{-2n})$. It has exactly the required simple poles and residues. Therefore all solutions are
$$

f(z)=h(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{-n}}{z-a^n},

$$
where $h$ is an arbitrary entire function.

Now suppose
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=a^n}f(z)=a^n.
$$
The naive series $\sum a^n/(z-a^n)$ does not converge, but the modified series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a^n}{z-a^n}+1\right)
$$
does converge normally on compact subsets away from the poles, because
$$
\frac{a^n}{z-a^n}+1
=\frac{z}{z-a^n}
=O(a^{-n})
$$
on compact sets. Therefore all solutions are
$$

f(z)=h(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a^n}{z-a^n}+1\right),

$$
where $h$ is an arbitrary entire function.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=6}
#problem

Prove that the sequence of entire functions
$$
f_n(z)=ze^{-nz^2}
$$
converges uniformly on $\mathbb R$, but does not converge normally, that is, uniformly on compact subsets, in the disk $|z|<r$ for any $r>0$.

#proof

For real $x$,
$$
|f_n(x)|=|x|e^{-nx^2}.
$$
The maximum occurs at $x=1/\sqrt{2n}$, and its value is
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}e^{-1/2}.
$$
Hence
$$
\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|f_n(x)|\to0.
$$
Thus $f_n\to0$ uniformly on $\mathbb R$.

Now fix any $r>0$ and choose $0<y<r$. Then $iy$ lies in the disk $|z|<r$, and
$$
f_n(iy)=iy\,e^{ny^2}.
$$
This tends to infinity in modulus as $n\to\infty$. Hence the sequence cannot converge uniformly on any compact subset containing $iy$. In particular, it does not converge normally in $|z|<r$ for any $r>0$.
::

::ProblemBlock{number=7}
#problem

Suppose $f$ is entire and there exists $A>0$ such that
$$
|f(z)|\le A(1+|z|)^{10}e^{10\operatorname{Im}z}
$$
for all $z\in\mathbb C$. Show that
$$
f(z)=P(z)e^{-i10z}
$$
for some polynomial $P(z)$ of degree at most $10$.

#proof

Define
$$
g(z)=f(z)e^{i10z}.
$$
Since
$$
|e^{i10z}|=e^{-10\operatorname{Im}z},
$$
we get
$$
|g(z)|
=|f(z)|e^{-10\operatorname{Im}z}
\le A(1+|z|)^{10}.
$$
Thus $g$ is an entire function of polynomial growth of degree at most $10$.

By Cauchy's estimates, all Taylor coefficients of $g$ of degree greater than $10$ vanish. Therefore $g=P$ is a polynomial of degree at most $10$. Hence
$$
f(z)=P(z)e^{-i10z}.
$$

::ProblemBlock{number=8}
#problem

Show that for all $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$,
$$
\pi\cot(\pi z)=\frac1z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}.
$$
Discuss the convergence of the series.

#proof

First note that
$$
\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}
=\frac1{z-n}+\frac1{z+n}.
$$
For $z$ in a compact subset of $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$,
$$
\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}=O\left(\frac1{n^2}\right)
$$
uniformly as $n\to\infty$. Therefore the series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$.

Define
$$
F(z)=\frac1z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}.
$$
Then $F$ is meromorphic with simple poles at the integers, and each pole has residue $1$. The function $\pi\cot(\pi z)$ also has simple poles at the integers, each with residue $1$. Hence
$$
H(z)=\pi\cot(\pi z)-F(z)
$$
has removable singularities at every integer, so $H$ is entire.

The symmetric partial sums
$$
\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\frac1{z-n}
$$
show that $F$ is periodic with period $1$ in the limit. Therefore $H$ is also periodic with period $1$.

On a vertical fundamental strip, both $\pi\cot(\pi z)$ and $F(z)$ are bounded away from small neighborhoods of the integers, and the singularities at the integers have already been removed in $H$. Hence $H$ is bounded on a fundamental strip. Since $H$ is periodic, it is bounded on all of $\mathbb C$. By Liouville's theorem, $H$ is constant.

Finally, both $\pi\cot(\pi z)$ and $F(z)$ are odd functions, so $H$ is odd. The only constant odd function is $0$. Therefore
$$
H\equiv0,
$$
which proves
$$

\pi\cot(\pi z)=\frac1z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}.

$$

::ProblemBlock{number=9}
#problem

Let $f$ be holomorphic on the disk $|z|<2$. Suppose there exists $N>1$ such that for all $r\in[1,2)$,
$$
\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\le r^N.
$$
Show that for some universal constant $C>0$, independent of $f$ and $N$,
$$
\max_{|z|=1}|f'(z)|\le CN^2.
$$

#proof

Fix $|z_0|=1$. Choose
$$
\delta=\frac1N.
$$
Since $N>1$, we have $0<\delta<1$, and the circle $|\zeta-z_0|=\delta$ lies inside $|\zeta|<1+\delta<2$.

By Cauchy's estimate,
$$
|f'(z_0)|
\le
\frac{1}{\delta}\max_{|\zeta-z_0|=\delta}|f(\zeta)|.
$$
For such $\zeta$,
$$
|\zeta|\le1+\delta=1+\frac1N.
$$
By the maximum modulus principle and the hypothesis,
$$
|f(\zeta)|\le \left(1+\frac1N\right)^N\le e.
$$
Therefore
$$
|f'(z_0)|\le eN.
$$
Since $N>1$, this implies
$$
|f'(z_0)|\le eN^2.
$$
Taking the maximum over $|z_0|=1$, the result follows with the universal constant $C=e$.
::
